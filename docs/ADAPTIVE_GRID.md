# Dynamic square grid

Urban update, 27 October 2026: connected development now preserves multiple centres, with wealth/prestige-dependent incorporation and eventual identity merger. Sparse block summaries and demand across several physical centres are described in [Urban centres and block detail](URBAN_CENTRES.md). This supersedes the immediate city-identity merging described in the earlier implementation notes below; numerical refinement and legal administration remain separate.

New worlds use `gridTopology: "square-v1"` and geography generator 5. The active numerical grid is a balanced quadtree covering land and water. Terrain samples are aligned 1,024 m squares (12 render units), centred at `(q + 0.5, r + 0.5)` sample spans. The root starts at `(0, 0)` and has a power-of-two number of sample spans. Every subdivision therefore has exactly the same outer boundary and area as its parent. There is no hex topology or staggered-row coordinate adapter.

Terrain samples retain stable identities for measured geography, resources, legal claims and surveyed transport waypoints. Numerical leaves have separate string addresses (`r`, `r0`, `r01`, …) and may aggregate many samples or subdivide one. Economic accounts and physical sites have their own stable identities. Splitting a numerical cell never divides a bank account, moves a building or creates inhabitants.

## Geometry and behavior

- Active leaves partition the physical domain exactly once, with no active ancestor and descendant together. Neighbouring leaf sizes differ by at most two. Source-by-source land and water area are conserved. Shared interfaces report exact land, water and coast contact lengths. Padding beyond the rectangular world is excluded.
- Density, workplaces, live-site workload (site count / 64), price variation, known terrain variation, coasts, river activity, established traffic and fleets drive refinement down to 128 m. Distinct split/merge thresholds, a twelve-month dwell period and a six-month scheduled remesh prevent numerical oscillation. Camera movement never changes simulation resolution. Refinement does not invent finer geological data.
- Aligned fragments contained in one child are reused instead of repeatedly clipped. Only fragments intersected by a subdivision allocate new polygons.
- Administrative contiguity uses shared faces. Roads and sailing may use diagonals, charged by physical distance; diagonal land/water transitions and travel between blocking corners are forbidden. Ports require a shared face. Fleet movement accounts for fractional progress along a diagonal and retains it in saves.
- River erosion, runoff accumulation and descending drainage are unchanged in method. Eight distance-weighted directions adapt that method to squares. Existing smooth reach, confluence, estuary and terrain-following rendering remains in place.
- Residential parcels and their occupants keep metre coordinates through every split/merge. Construction and relocation remain funded economic actions. Spatial queries use the tree, then exact physical distance/geometry; numerical size is never free travel or new construction.
- The same square footprints serve physical access, map boundaries and point selection. Coasts retain presentation smoothing; that smoothing does not change the authoritative land mask. The old whole-cell urban tint is removed; settlement scenery supplies the developed footprint.

## APIs

| API                                                                   | Purpose                                                                                          |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `adaptiveMesh(world)`                                                 | Reconstruct/read the current authoritative partition and derived totals                          |
| `adaptiveGrid(world)`                                                 | Read leaves, ID lookup and symmetric physical adjacency                                          |
| `locateAdaptiveCell(world, point)`                                    | Resolve a meter position to an active leaf and exact source fragment; reject outside-domain gaps |
| `queryAdaptiveLeaves(world, bounds)`                                  | Recursively select intersecting leaves                                                           |
| `queryAdaptiveSitesInBounds(world, bounds, predicate?)`               | Select exact live site positions within a rectangle                                              |
| `queryAdaptiveSites(world, point, radius, predicate?)`                | Select exact live sites within a radius                                                          |
| `nearestAdaptiveSites(world, point, count, predicate?, maxDistance?)` | Find nearest qualifying sites with branch-and-bound pruning                                      |
| `refreshAdaptiveTotals(world)`                                        | Refresh membership/totals after authoritative site changes, without changing the partition       |
| `commuteSiteRoute(world, from, to, limit?)`                           | Compute access using physical site positions and surveyed links                                  |

Call the membership refresh after adding or moving sites before querying their new positions. Queries are read-only and never initialize new housing or rewrite saved tree state. Returned site objects belong to the authoritative world; callers must not mutate them in inspection code.

## Saves and storage

This release requires newly generated square worlds, as explicitly chosen for the topology change. Saves without `square-v1` are rejected before entering the simulation, with a message to create a new world. They are never silently interpreted as squares.

The production disk namespace is `.uchrony/autosaves/square-v1/`, and the browser database is `uchrony-square-saves`. Earlier disk files and the `uchrony-saves` database remain untouched. Square saves preserve the complete land/water tree, hysteresis, physical sites, ownership and fractional sailing progress. Load validation checks the grid marker, exact sample centres, fixed metre scale, full partition coverage and traversable routes.

## Parallel influence and dense settlements

The housing solver now builds immutable work/service influence snapshots without city entities. A separate source quadtree inside each terrain sample aggregates coincident workplaces exactly and accepts distant branches under a 2% relative kernel-error bound. Reverse network search establishes accessible source cells before any aggregation; urban density, borders and water remain physical constraints. The source tree is independent of the current numerical partition.

Work is split into batches of at most 64 homes, including multiple batches inside a single terrain cell. A shared atomic queue distributes those batches across up to 32 workers, bounded by available hardware and workload. Input arrays are shared once, outputs have one writer, and reduction order is identical across worker counts. The owner worker waits for every batch to finish before applying housing decisions; idle dispatched workers do not delay an already complete field. Worker modules warm up asynchronously before playback. A worker failure or ten seconds without batch progress discards partial output and disables the pool in favor of the identical serial calculation. The UI thread never waits on this barrier. The local dev and preview servers provide the cross-origin isolation headers required for shared memory; other hosts must do the same to enable this path.

Home destinations are ranked once per pool rather than sorted for every origin. Connected-footprint discovery removes visited sites from per-leaf candidate sets, avoiding repeated scans through already consumed urban neighborhoods. City labels are an observer projection; physical density and paid housing development govern urban shares and attraction.

`tests/settlement-influence.test.ts` covers exhaustive field-error bounds, 100,000 coincident sources, real worker-count equivalence, failure recovery, city-label independence, funded service attraction and complete monthly/save continuation. `node --import tsx tests/qualify-influence.ts` records dense-field and World-scale field measurements. The million-home case is a numeric workload, not a claim that the full economy can advance a million independent household accounts at the same speed. The exact financial, cohort, trade, political and military phases remain serial.

## Map-first urban detail — 26 October 2026

The initial display now prepares the same physical world used by playback, without advancing a month. UI snapshots carry leaf addresses plus three packed numeric arrays (resident totals, jobs, land area), and compact home records carry developed status and pending capacity. They still omit accounts, mesh fragments, work buffers and the mutable site registry. Visible-area traversal reconstructs bounds from real tree addresses; drawing and picking never remesh the world.

Occupied developed parcels and paid construction drive refinement across their complete physical bounds, including places below the settlement naming threshold. The target span is `max(minimumSpan, min(512, parcelSpan / (1 + sqrt(residents / 12000))))` metres. Population and employment totals retain their authoritative site positions; overlapping development supports are refinement signals only and cannot duplicate residents or assets. Existing 2:1 balance and six-month remeshing remain. Dwell checks now include descendant changes, so an ancestor cannot collapse a recently split subtree prematurely.

The default Quadtree view shows actual cells, density-shaded urban plots, vacant stock and construction outlines. Its Population layer uses residents per km² of each leaf's land area. A hover highlights the cell; the province context can inspect its exact bounds. 3D is initialized on demand and disposed when returning to Quadtree. Plot geometry and capacity continue to come from funded construction; occupancy follows the existing migration/location mechanisms, and cities remain labels of connected occupied development. There is no simulated building census or new demolition process. Opening housing still uses its recorded coarse physical support, and terrain knowledge remains at 1,024 m samples.

`tests/quadtree-map.test.ts` checks month-zero validity, projection fidelity, exact picking, viewport queries, unnamed development, vacancies, pending works, full-footprint refinement, vacancy hysteresis and physical invariance under remeshing. The isolated `/tests/browser/quadtree.html` fixture advances real monthly simulation without saving or model calls. ## Frontier settlement and building lots

New worlds created in the app use `settlementStart: "frontier"`. Each state begins with one supplied settlement of 2,500 residents and 500 treasury; most provinces remain genuinely unclaimed (`nation: -1`). Roughly one in seven unclaimed provinces has a small independent rural population; the others are empty. Existing settled saves are retained. The generator's optional `settled` mode remains available to reproduce existing test fixtures.

Founding moves 600 dated residents and their proportional deposits, liabilities and ownership claims from an accessible source pool, retaining at least 1,200 residents there. A camp costs 120 treasury, 40 timber, 20 stone and 5 tools, and carries 94 food; the source also retains three months of food. Founding is limited to nearby land passage through home or unclaimed territory. The current sovereignty unit remains a province, so founding claims that surrounding province, not an arbitrary quadtree leaf. Autonomous states use a smaller funded 120-person expedition and review eligible adjacent land quarterly (earlier under reported peer pressure), retaining 300 residents and half a month of food at home. Their range is 40 cells; manual expeditions retain the larger requirements above. Independent communities use local production, trade and demographic rules without a government account or automatic state membership.

Frontier housing has persistent building footprints (`buildingVersion: 1`). The initial 26 October implementation used 16 m lots; new surveys now use the varied frontage layouts described below. Rural homes remain scattered. Subdivision preserves every resident, capacity, opening capital and paid construction commitment. It creates no household accounts, markets, firms or money: production, prices, taxes, dated populations and accounts retain their larger economic pools. House locations and the urban classifier use the finer physical records.

Occupied buildings and funded works refine the numerical quadtree to 8 m. Buildings keep their exact geometry when numerical cells split or merge, and empty structures can remain after the numerical mesh coarsens. New housing compares rebuilding with paid construction on a new lot; additions have material and labor costs and a three-month build lag. City names follow connected occupied building clusters (up to 24 m between footprints, with real walking access). Vacant capacity cannot bridge two settlements. Labels and administrative boundaries do not create urban fabric.

The flat map draws the actual footprints and picks individual buildings; the 3D view uses those same footprints and capacities. Older decorative activity props are suppressed in building-scale worlds so they cannot invent additional homes, workplaces or people on top of recorded lots. Building-scale zoom uses metre labels and frames the complete physical cluster in both presentations. No rectangular city envelope is filled. Older settled saves retain their coarse housing supports; frontier saves are upgraded conservatively once. This supersedes the coarse housing description in the earlier map-first pass above. Terrain sampling remains 1,024 m: the finer building grid does not invent finer geology.

`tests/frontier-settlements.test.ts` covers opening conditions, paid founding, rejected orders, independent towns, autonomous decisions and deterministic save continuation. `tests/building-lots.test.ts` covers conservation, unchanged economic pools, 8 m leaves, building picking, persistent geometry, occupied-lot mergers and funded outward surveys. See [frontier-building-lots-2026-10-26.json](validation/frontier-building-lots-2026-10-26.json) for bounded validation, including the remaining large-map cost.

## Organic frontage and ground roads — 27 October 2026

New opening settlements and funded expansion survey free-positioned plots beside branching lanes. Buildings face the adjacent lane with varied widths, depths, setbacks and capacities; parcels no longer snap to a fixed lattice. Larger and smaller homes share frontages, with more floor capacity near the opening centre and more open spacing outward. A conservative interpolation of recorded elevations guides streets along contours. Surveys reject water, obstructed access, steep building supports, overlapping footprints and blocked street frontages. Actual freight roads can establish orientation and provide additional frontage as the world develops.

Opening subdivision apportions the existing housing stock and commitments by footprint and centrality weights, preserving totals. Later expansion still competes with infill and pays the ordinary material, wage and access costs. Surveying is read-only; lanes and new footprints are recorded only when a founding or building transaction is adopted. Existing buildings are never moved by a camera change, remesh, save load or this update. Older building saves without lanes can survey access from outside their occupied footprints. The coarse economy remains unchanged.

Local lanes and known land-trade edges are painted as dirt into the map canvas and the relief terrain overlay. Shared edges are deduplicated, sea routes remain shipping lanes, and no raised road pieces are built. Physical tracks remain visible when the freight overlay is off; the explicit Trade layer may add quantity emphasis. These are procedural ground textures, with no external raster assets. Terrain and markets still retain their previous source resolutions; interpolated gradients are a siting heuristic, not new geological evidence.

Validation, screenshots and the six-month browser run are recorded in [organic-growth-2026-10-27.json](validation/organic-growth-2026-10-27.json). Existing performance and economic-calibration limitations still apply.

## High-mountain exclusion — 27 October 2026

The mountain biome (or a recorded elevation above 0.43, including stale biome labels) is now a hard residential and movement exclusion. Hills and low passes remain usable. Generation, founding, building surveys, walking access, commuting, freight and land migration share this constraint. Diagonal movement cannot cut a mountain corner; road and harbor graphs omit blocked edges rather than assigning them a larger finite cost. A complete ridge can disconnect a journey. Existing corridor familiarity and path inertia cannot reopen it.

Mountains receive no opening residents or new housing, including the fallback allocation for first arrivals into empty territory. Crop textures and activity props also exclude them. Administrative boundaries can still cross a ridge: they are ownership records, not roads. Terrain classification retains the existing 1,024 m source resolution.

Authoritative preparation performs a versioned correction for older adaptive saves. Invalid mountain housing occupancy, capacity, capital and pending works are consolidated into the nearest usable housing records, normally within the same economic pool. A wholly alpine pool transfers its dated communities, deposits, liabilities and ownership claims to an eligible pool, preferring the same province and state. This is an explicit correction of the former terrain assumption, with no births, deaths, free money or production. The old zero-occupancy home records remain for ownership compatibility. Invalid route plans and current route summaries are withdrawn; historical shipment accounting remains intact. Read-only map queries do not perform this migration.

`tests/mountain-access.test.ts` covers closed ridges, low passes, blocked corners and ports, zero mountain residents in both start modes after monthly simulation and save restoration, legacy census/asset conservation, first-arrival allocation, and map texture/route exclusions. The isolated quadtree fixture has an “Explore mountains” control and accepts `?start=settled` to inspect the populated landscape. [Validation and browser evidence](validation/mountain-exclusion-2026-10-27.json) includes a six-month settled-world run with 308 mountain cells, zero mountain residents and zero routes crossing blocked terrain.

Completed shipment records can retain a former mountain market endpoint after the terrain correction moves that district's center. Save validation accepts that historical endpoint only with the terrain migration marker, an excluded peak still inside the same district, and a habitable replacement center. Intermediate geometry and all other endpoint checks remain enforced. This preserves shipment quantities, accounts and exact recorded paths without admitting new mountain travel or rewriting history. A regression exposed by a real March 1725 checkpoint is covered by the migration/save round-trip test.

Save-writer replacement retains the one captured immutable snapshot and retries only unconfirmed replicas, with at most three sends and a fixed 90-second response deadline. Stale writer events cannot disconnect a replacement. Shared worker failures are reported once with their underlying error. Validation and live-save evidence are recorded in [save-recovery-2026-10-27.json](validation/save-recovery-2026-10-27.json).
