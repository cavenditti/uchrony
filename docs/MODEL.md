# Simulation model

Uchrony's model is deliberately inspectable. These are implemented rules, not claims about real-world predictive accuracy. Coefficients are synthetic. Registered settings are centralized in `src/sim/configuration-defaults.ts` and validated by the configuration handler.

## Configuration and scenario boundaries

The new-world dialog has Basic and Advanced views. Advanced exposes the registered model values, a frontier/settled starting pattern, opening endowments and generation adjustments. The world menu’s Simulation parameters editor stages a batch, validates it in full and applies it at the next synchronous command boundary. Closing the editor discards pending live edits. Search, section resets, and JSON parameter presets use the same schema as save validation; presets describe parameters, while the world’s seed, size and starting pattern are chosen separately.

Starting conditions appear first, with separate opening treasuries for Frontier and Established starts. These amounts are thousands of crowns; the setup preview expands the active amount into crowns. Settlement expedition cash is an expense paid during play, not an opening cash grant. Every parameter has a question-mark explanation available by hover, keyboard focus or tap, including each biome matrix cell. Explanations live in `configuration-help.ts`; registration rejects parameters without help so new controls cannot silently lose this coverage.

`configuration-defaults.ts` owns calibration data; `configuration-schema.ts` owns bounds, labels, enums and creation-only flags; `configuration.ts` owns normalization, compatibility, revision checks and atomic application. Persisted values are versioned and immutable after validation. World generation, ticks, commands, save validation and display snapshots select a synchronous world-specific scope in `configuration-context.ts`. Pure calculation helpers may use the scoped compatibility views, but callers outside those boundaries must enter `withWorldConfiguration` or read `configurationFor(world)` explicitly. Never carry a scope across `await`, and never cache derived tunable values at module load. Existing decision and expansion experiment overrides remain separate, explicit synchronous scopes.

Creation-only controls include geography, opening balances, bank count, age-class boundaries, opening prices and the built-density conversion. Live edits change future flows and decisions. Existing people, physical stock, loans, funded projects and monetary observation history are preserved. An explicit policy-rate edit changes the current rate; the monetary rule may change it in later months. A stale revision or invalid batch changes nothing. Structural units, encoding dimensions and safety limits are centralized in `simulation-invariants.ts`; these need migrations or recomputation, not live editing. Algorithmic literals still remain in simulation modules; the registry is the required destination for further calibration extraction, not a claim that every numeric expression is configurable.

Climate uses a typed five-by-four temperature/moisture matrix, with exhaustive temperature-band overrides for desert and tundra. Thresholds, every matrix entry, seasonal amplitudes, crop mappings and growth normalization are configuration data. Boundary inclusivity preserves the previous model, including the exact −5, 7, 14 and 22 °C and moisture 0.2, 0.48 and 0.55 boundaries; no competing biome-rule order determines the result. Calendar months and the hemispheric half-year offset remain calendar identities.

## Resolution and units

- Time advances by one month per tick, beginning in January 1720. The starting year is a fictional scenario convention, not a historical dataset.
- New browser worlds default to 448 × 288 square terrain samples, about 1,400 provinces, and 32 states. The new-world dialog also offers Regional (112 × 72 / ~180 provinces) and Continental (224 × 144 / ~560 provinces), with 4–48 starting states. Disconnected islands can add provinces. Terrain samples retain their physical size; greater dimensions add actual travel distance rather than merely refining the same extent. Aligned squares carry fixed physical geography; active numerical cells split and merge on the same dyadic coordinates. Permanent local market and household records own economic state. A separate physical quadtree groups that state, while dated municipalities and provinces describe current administration. On the first monthly step, a legacy or newly generated provincial opening balance sheet is conservatively allocated into districts. Square-world saves retain their dimensions. Pre-square worlds are rejected with a request to generate a new world; their saved files are not converted or overwritten. Direct `generateWorld` calls default to Regional for compatibility with existing callers and small fixtures; the worker explicitly selects the browser default.
- Population values are people. Stocks and flows are tonnes. Treasury, budget, and the internal `gdp` field are thousands of fictional crowns; the UI expands them into crowns.
- The UI reports GDP for the modeled economy, reconciled by production, expenditure and income. It includes goods, trade margins, freight, public services and construction. Reference-price goods output remains a separate quantity indicator. These are synthetic national accounts, not an empirically calibrated national GDP series; housing, imputed financial services and many real-world service industries are outside the model.
- Terrain samples have 1,024 m sides and 12 internal map units per side, or 1,024/12 meters per map unit. Face neighbors are 1,024 m apart; diagonal travel costs the corresponding Euclidean distance, and land/water corner shortcuts are prohibited. This explicit synthetic scale is used by commuting, freight and the map legend; it is not an empirical calibration.
- Generating the same seed with the same settings on the same model version produces the same world. The PRNG state is part of every save.

## Money and balance sheets

Index t denotes a month, r a resident market district (a province before local initialization), s an economic stratum, f a firm, g a good, b a bank and a a customer account. Monetary quantities are thousands of crowns; prices are crowns per tonne. Divide price × tonnes by 1,000 for payments. Labor is full-time-equivalent person-months.

```text
payment = min(requested payment, payer deposits)
closing deposits[a] = opening deposits[a] + receipts − payments + creation − destruction
sum(customer deposit changes) = deposits created − deposits destroyed
bank assets = reserves + loan claims
bank liabilities and equity = customer deposits + central-bank advances + equity
central-bank assets = advances to banks
central-bank liabilities and equity = bank reserves + central-bank equity
closing goods = opening goods + production + explicit injections
                − intermediate use − final use − spoilage − destruction
```

Households, firms, district wholesalers, governments, provincial administrations and institutions hold deposits. By default three banks share one currency and central bank; the count can be selected during world creation. Payments between banks transfer reserves; payments inside one bank reassign its deposit liabilities. Every customer balance and bank balance sheet is checked each month. The journal keeps the latest 1,024 entries and complete monthly net changes, including separately identified creation and destruction. Military inventories are included in goods accounting.

Opening deposits and bank equity have matching reserve assets. The initial reserve endowment is matched by negative opening central-bank equity; it is an explicit initial condition, not an invented government bond or current income. The former credit pool becomes opening bank equity when an older economic save is upgraded. Existing loans become opening bank assets, with matching opening equity and unchanged borrower liabilities. There is no foreign currency, external rest-of-world sector or asset exchange. Household cash remains with its family stratum after death; an empty stratum transfers its estate to a surviving local stratum.

## Deposit-creating banks and monetary policy

```text
new credit = min(request, borrower limit − outstanding debt,
                 max(0, bank equity / 0.10 − bank loan assets))
origination: borrower deposit +L; borrower debt +L;
             bank loan asset +L; bank deposit liability +L
principal repayment: deposit −R; debt −R; bank loan asset −R; deposit liability −R
interest paid: customer deposit −J; bank deposit liability −J; bank equity +J
bank dividend: recipient deposit +D; bank deposit liability +D; bank equity −D
loan write-down: borrower debt −W; bank loan asset −W; bank equity −W
```

Lending creates deposits rather than transferring an existing pool. Banks require 10% equity against loan assets. Firm working-capital debt is limited to 30% of expected monthly sales divided by the monthly interest-plus-principal-service rate, for both payroll and material procurement. Wholesalers retain their synthetic limit of max(1, six months of local subsistence sales); governments retain max(1,000, 24 × measured monthly GDP). Bank capital can constrain lending further. These are underwriting assumptions, not historically estimated credit standards.

New loans float at the policy rate plus 0.15% per month, resetting every three months. Existing legacy loans remain fixed. New borrowing on an existing facility blends its current rate with the rate on the additional principal; a floating facility subsequently resets as a whole on its contractual date. A fixed facility retains the blended rate until another top-up. This pooled-facility approximation does not track individual vintages or contractual maturity. Reset terms, principal, lender, arrears and origination dates survive district splits and save restoration. Scheduled principal repayment is 1.5% per month, bounded by cash after interest. No rate change itself creates deposits or income.

Twelve consecutive months of unpaid interest trigger a 65% principal write-down. Full interest payment resets the counter. Sovereign restructuring reduces legitimacy by four and stability by three. Financial default does not destroy physical inventories or capital. Firms in persistent distress liquidate: available cash repays creditors first, unrecovered loans are written down, remaining goods and equipment transfer to local shared capacity, and residual cash belongs to owners.

A solvent bank can obtain central-bank reserves for an outgoing settlement, limited to 95% of its loan assets less existing advances. This creates both reserves and a central-bank claim, without increasing commercial-bank equity. Advances pay the policy rate; excess reserves above 10% of deposits repay them. An insolvent bank wipes out existing ownership and converts enough deposit claims into equity to restore the 10% loan-capital target, bounded by outstanding deposits. Conversion is proportional across depositors, who receive explicit ownership shares. It destroys deposits and is reported separately; there is no free recapitalization or deposit guarantee. If deposit conversion is exhausted, any remaining central-bank claim is written down with a matching loss in central-bank equity. This recognizes a creditor loss; it issues no new deposits. Bank income is interest received less central-bank charges and credit losses. Up to 65% of positive monthly income can be distributed, subject to a 12.5% loan-capital buffer; losses reduce both income and equity. Enterprise liquidations after bank settlement enter the next settlement’s retained capital constraint.

```text
monthly price change = log(P[t] / P[t−1])
inflation π = sum(last 12 monthly log changes)
expected inflation += (π − expected inflation) / 24
annual desired policy rate i* = r* + π + 0.5 × (π − π*) + (u* − u)
next annual policy rate = max(0, 0.85 × current annual rate + 0.15 × i*)
monthly loan/advance policy rate = annual policy rate / 12
r* = 0.02; π* = 0.02; u* = 0.04
```

The policy announcement follows completed monthly GDP, price and employment measurement; it affects subsequent decisions. Initial missing price history uses the prior expected inflation for each missing month. Expectations start at 2%; legacy saves start a new observation window. Annual nominal rates are quoted as twelve times the monthly contractual charge; inflation is a log change. There is no upper policy-rate cap. The zero lower bound is an explicit regime assumption. This inertial inflation-target rule follows the structure of the [Federal Reserve's illustrative policy rules](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/2026-07-mpr-part2.htm); it is not a reconstruction of the historical Federal Reserve, a full monetary equilibrium, or an estimated reaction function.

Policy affects loan repricing, debt-service affordability, inventory carrying costs and the real financing hurdle for investment. Deposits remain created by lending and extinguished by principal repayment, with interest, distributions and bank losses booked explicitly. This mechanism follows the distinction explained in the [Bank of England's money-creation paper](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy). The model still omits deposit interest, reserve remuneration, interbank unsecured loans, bond trading, deposit insurance and imputed financial-service output. Loan interest is property income, excluded from GDP; no financial-service charge is inferred from the interest spread.

## United States historical research benchmark: 1960–1985

The first historical experiment uses frozen BEA real GDP, BLS CPI, unemployment, civilian labor force and domestic crude-petroleum prices, and Federal Reserve effective policy rates, obtained from [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/). Dataset files preserve source URLs, units, retrieval date and hashes. These are revised observations, not historical real-time vintages. Monthly observations become quarterly arithmetic means; GDP remains a quarterly seasonally adjusted annual-rate level. Annual inflation is the four-quarter log CPI change; growth is four times quarterly log GDP change.

This is a **separate quarterly aggregate research model**, sharing the spatial world's job-retention, matching and policy-rate functions. It does not simulate the US map, individual banks, wage bargaining or the transaction/GDP ledger. It supplies a falsifiable macroeconomic benchmark while those fuller historical initial conditions remain unimplemented. The generated world has no explicit petroleum sector or historical US institutional reconstruction.

Forty quarters in 1960–1969 fit a fixed 81-candidate grid for demand persistence, interest sensitivity, the Phillips slope and expectations adjustment. The objective is squared growth RMSE divided by 4², plus squared inflation and unemployment RMSE each divided by 2², all in percentage points. Potential output and labor supply use log-linear trends estimated only on those forty quarters; natural unemployment and the neutral real rate use their training means. Energy pass-through (0.04), energy demand effect (0.025), matching efficiency (0.65), quarterly separations (1−0.985³), and employment elasticity (0.5) remain declared assumptions. Calm 1960s energy prices cannot identify the later oil-crisis response reliably.

```text
x[t] = ρ x[t−1] − σ × (i[t−1] − πe[t−1] − r*)/4 − χ Δlog(oil[t])
potential log GDP[t] = fitted intercept + fitted trend × quarter
desired employment = min(L, L × (1−u*) × exp(0.5 × x[t]))
retained employment = min(L, desired employment, previous employment × (1−separation))
vacancies = desired employment − retained employment
hires = min(L−retained, vacancies, 0.65 × sqrt((L−retained) × vacancies))
u[t] = 1 − (retained + hires) / L
quarterly log inflation = (πe[t−1] + κ × (u*−u[t]))/4 + γ Δlog(oil[t])
annual inflation = sum(last four quarterly log changes)
πe[t] = πe[t−1] + λ × (annual inflation − πe[t−1])
log GDP[t] = potential log GDP[t] + x[t]
```

Here x is the log output gap; i is the annualized nominal policy rate; πe is annual expected log inflation; L grows at its training trend. The initial four-quarter inflation window is initialized evenly from the observed annual rate. The comparison begins from the common observed 1969Q4 state and runs for 64 quarters without resetting to later outcomes. Subsequent GDP, CPI, unemployment and labor force are scoring data only. Four experiments combine recorded versus rule-based policy with recorded versus constant energy prices. The policy-rule experiment uses the world rule's form with quarterly inertia 0.6 and a modern 2% target; it does not represent the historical Federal Reserve.

Event annotations identify the [1973–74 embargo](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/oil-shock-of-1973-74), [1978–79 oil shock](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/oil-shock-of-1978-79) and [October 1979 monetary-policy change](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/anti-inflation-measures). Replayed policy and domestic oil prices are conditional paths with endogenous historical components, not statistically identified shocks. Paired experiments measure this model's responses, not the historical causal contribution of each event. Half and one-and-a-half energy-coefficient runs report sensitivity, not confidence intervals.

The first holdout **fails historical qualification**. RMSE in percentage points:

| Experiment, 1970–1985                         | GDP growth | Annual inflation | Unemployment |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------: | ---------------: | -----------: |
| Recorded policy and energy                    |       9.26 |            24.31 |        10.85 |
| Modern policy rule and recorded energy        |       4.79 |             4.03 |         1.96 |
| Simple 1969-origin persistence/drift baseline |       4.60 |             3.14 |         3.68 |

The declared criterion requires beating that baseline on all three targets. The baseline persists 1969Q4 inflation and unemployment and extrapolates mean 1960s GDP growth. Recorded-rate replay develops excessive deflation and unemployment: its interest-rate path cannot respond to the model's own divergent inflation expectations. The policy-rule experiment improves unemployment fit but misses growth and inflation. Neither result validates the spatial engine. Missing productivity/participation shifts, fiscal and external demand, wage-price controls, exchange-rate regimes, sectoral energy substitution and historical expectations are material limitations. This failed experiment is retained; fitting parameters to the displayed holdout would require a new independent evaluation period.

The reproducible command is `npm run evaluate:history`; its report contains every quarterly observation, every simulated path, MAE/RMSE/bias, GDP-level errors, sensitivity runs, paired energy responses, data hashes and source fingerprint. This evaluation is independent of accounting-conservation and long-run endurance checks. The earlier 200-year report applies to model version 4 and does not qualify version 5.

## Demographic equations

A birth cohort is (absolute birth month, female count, male count). Age in years is (t − birth month)/12. Broad display ages are derived from that date; there are no memoryless fractions moving between three age pools.

```text
age < 1: annual hazard h = 0.055
ages 1–14: annual hazard h = 0.002
ages 15+: h = 0.0015 + 0.00018 × exp(0.085 × max(0, age−30))
adjusted hazard = [h × (1.5−health) + max(0,shortage)² × vulnerability] × mortality effect
vulnerability = 0.18 before age 5; 0.22 from age 65; 0.10 otherwise
monthly death probability = 1 − exp(−adjusted hazard / 12)
expected deaths = cohort count × monthly death probability
desired surviving children D = 2.3 + 1.2 × (1−literacy) × (1−urbanization)
                                  − 0.6 × urbanization × literacy
child survival S = exp(−(0.055 + 14 × 0.002) × (1.5−health))
planned lifetime fertility T = D / S
reference lifetime fertility T0 = 5 × sum(age-specific fertility coefficients) = 2.505
expected births = sum(females in age band × annual fertility / 12)
                  × T/T0 × (1−0.8 food shortage) × historical fertility effect
```

Annual fertility coefficients for ages 15–19, 20–24, 25–29, 30–34, 35–39, 40–44 and 45–49 are 0.018, 0.115, 0.145, 0.120, 0.075, 0.025 and 0.003 births per woman. The female newborn share is 0.488. Maximum modeled age is 110; remaining members die at that boundary. Expected fractional counts use floor(x) plus a seeded Bernoulli draw with probability x−floor(x), not a full individual-level binomial model. These coefficients describe a synthetic scenario and are not fitted UN estimates.

In adaptive worlds, the fertility equation's urbanization input is an opportunity-cost proxy: `min(1, connected footprint population / 50000)`, weighted by actual occupied residents into their district. Unnamed camps participate; the physical component calculation does not read city names, IDs or the city catalogue. Rural residents outside compact footprints contribute zero. This separates small agricultural settlements from large urban concentrations without changing their physical urban census. The 50,000-person scale is a synthetic tuning parameter. The baseline experimental profile and non-adaptive worlds retain the previous urban-share input.

Family preferences represent rural child-labor and old-age-security motives, urban opportunity costs and expected childhood survival. They do not target the current population, replace this month's deaths or force a net reproduction rate of one. Literate urban populations can remain below replacement; famine, displacement and age structure can still cause decline. The age pattern is normalized to the planned lifetime fertility, rather than multiplying an already low schedule by permanent education and urban penalties. These are transparent behavioral assumptions, not estimates of any real population. The [UN definition of net reproduction](https://data.un.org/Glossary.aspx?q=Net+reproduction+rate%20%28surviving%20daughters%20per%20woman%29) distinguishes surviving daughters from births and explains why mortality matters to replacement.

The convex shortage term distinguishes a mild ration shortfall from severe deprivation. It is a synthetic hazard function, not a fitted medical dose-response curve. The model retains each stratum's affordability and actual delivered food; it cannot infer nutritional health from national food production alone. [FAO's dietary-energy framework](https://www.fao.org/measuring-hunger/access-to-dietary-energy/computing-the-pou/) similarly distinguishes food availability, requirements and unequal access. Individual calories, body mass, food quality and within-stratum nutritional inequality are not represented.

## Strata, labor and wages

Every cultural/language/belief/political community can contain smallholders, laborers, artisans, professionals and owners. Each district-stratum has pooled cash, income, taxes, transfers, purchases and employment. Strata do not confer cultural or ethnic abilities. They represent economic roles with wage multipliers 0.90, 1.00, 1.25, 1.70 and 0.80 respectively; owners also receive dividends. When initializing enterprises, an inhabited district starts with min(3, ceil(population / 10,000)) independently financed firms and one dormant slot. Empty districts start without firms. Existing firms in legacy saves keep their identities and sites. Subsequent boundary changes never create firms. Firms have separate deposits, credit, inventories, productivity, wage offers, markups, employment, capital and construction projects. A district wholesaler connects firms to household, public and interdistrict demand. A reopened slot increments its generation; newly financed enterprises receive new persistent IDs.

```text
participation(age) = 0 below 18; 0.60 at 18–24; 0.78 at 25–59;
                    0.50 at 60–64; 0.15 at 65–69; 0 from 70
labor force = sum(cohort count × participation × (0.6+0.4 health))
forecast[f,g,t] = 0.82 forecast[f,g,t−1] + 0.18 observed sales[f,g,t−1]
product demand = max(firm forecast, wholesaler sales forecast / active firms,
                     local subsistence/input orders / active firms)
product plan = max(0, demand + 0.25 × (2 × demand − own stock))
executable plan[g] = min(plan[g], available recipe input / (recipe coefficient × active firms))
required labor share[g] = base share[g] × clamp(executable plan[g] / benchmark output[g], 0, 4)
utilization = clamp(sum(required shares), 0.05, 0.98)
product labor mix = required shares / sum(required shares)
public service share = (education + military + research + 30) /
                       (education + military + research + welfare + 30)
anticipated public payroll = min(local population share of treasury,
                                labor force × mean wage × collected tax rate × service share)
private labor = max(0, labor force − anticipated public payroll / mean wage)
desired jobs = private labor / active firms × utilization × (1−0.5 devastation)
consumption c = 0.7 × previous food coverage + 0.3 × previous material coverage
reservation b = 0.40758 × basket cost per skill-adjusted worker × c²
productive value y = benchmark output value less recipe inputs, per skill-adjusted worker
bargaining share β = 0.5 × previous funded employment / previous labor force
negotiated wage = b + β × max(0, y − b)
wage[t] = (11/12) × wage[t−1] × exp(expected annual log inflation / 12)
          + (1/12) × negotiated wage
retained jobs = min(funded desired jobs, previous jobs × (1−0.015))
vacancies V = max(0, funded desired jobs − retained jobs)
new matches M = min(U, V, 0.65 × U^0.5 × V^0.5)
employment = retained jobs + hires
```

Reservation compensation follows the marginal rate of substitution under CRRA consumption utility (risk aversion 2) and linear work disutility: with utility derivative c^(−2), compensation for an extra unit of work is proportional to c². The subsistence basket supplies the consumption unit; it is **not guaranteed outside income**. Deprivation increases marginal utility and lowers reservation compensation. Work disutility 0.40758 and the 70/30 coverage proxy are synthetic assumptions. There is no fixed nominal wage floor. Productive value uses benchmark average value added rather than a differentiated marginal product or solved firm value function. Firms with productive value below reservation compensation do not open jobs. Existing nominal wages renegotiate gradually; new hires require an offer at least as high as reservation compensation.

Private contracts persist across months. Desired downsizing, separation and inability to pay reduce retained staff. If migration, retirement or boundary changes reduce the resident pool, retained claims scale together to fit it. Remaining vacancies match remaining job seekers; firms receive matches in proportion to their funded vacancies. Wage differences enter acceptance and funding, rather than letting a high offer confiscate another firm's retained staff. Staff are aggregate local claims, not named individual employment histories; allocation across strata follows the resident labor-force shares. Each firm records opening staff, retained jobs, separations, hires, vacancies and unfilled openings. Wages settle through actual transfers before output. The [search-and-matching literature](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/advanced-economicsciences2010.pdf) motivates the matching law; this model does not solve the full Diamond–Mortensen–Pissarides free-entry and surplus equations.

Private, public and construction jobs share one labor-force ceiling. Public hiring reserves only a treasury-backed expectation; unused reservations remain unemployment. Public and construction employment remain monthly purchased services, not persistent civil-service contracts. Public services are valued at actual labor cost. Reference-price output uses base wage 0.0015 times the skill multiplier, so wage inflation alone creates no real services. Unemployment is one minus employed labor divided by participating labor, rather than the entire population.

Slow occupational mobility uses probability 0.0005 × opportunity per month: urban smallholders can become laborers; literate laborers artisans; sufficiently literate artisans professionals; wealthy professionals owners. Severe deprivation can push artisans, professionals or owners back into laboring work. Exact age/sex profiles and proportional cash move together. This is a reduced-form family-group transition model, not individual schooling, inheritance or occupational licensing.

## Household choice and deprivation

Let γ[g] be the subsistence quantity, p[g] the price in thousands of crowns per tonne and B the cash-constrained monthly budget. The discretionary shares β are food 0.50, timber 0.08, stone 0.02, iron 0.01, coal 0.08, tools 0.10, textiles 0.20, arms 0.01, summing to one.

```text
subsistence cost S = sum(p[g] × γ[g])
disposable income Y = wages + dividends + freight receipts + transfers − taxes
B = min(cash, max(S, 0.85 × max(0,Y) + 0.05 × max(0,cash−3S)))
desired quantity[g] = γ[g] + β[g] × (B−S) / p[g]     when B ≥ S
delivered quantity = min(desired quantity, available stock, remaining cash / price)
```

Above subsistence the starting demand rule is the linear expenditure system associated with Stone–Geary preferences. In model 6, food quantity is capped at 1.5 times subsistence and its released spending is redistributed to nonfood goods in proportion to their discretionary shares. When B < S the usual subsistence utility has no feasible solution: the implementation explicitly switches to priority rationing, purchasing food first, then timber, stone, iron, coal, tools, textiles and arms. All strata submit their affordable orders together. At each seller quote, the available lot is allocated in proportion to those orders. Sellers clear from cheapest to most expensive; goods retain the food-first ordering. Array position no longer gives a stratum first claim to the food supply. This is quantity rationing at posted prices, not a Walrasian price auction; wealth and desired quantities can still produce unequal access. Unspent money remains with households.

Wholesalers withhold at most half their nonfood stock against forecast workshop-input orders. Food is excluded from that reserve. This preserves a physical route for tools, weapons and other production chains to restart after a stockout.

For each good, households compare available local wholesaler and firm offers in ascending price order. The demand calculation uses the cheapest currently available offer; actual purchases respect each seller's stock, the household's remaining budget and its deposits. Direct firm purchases transfer payment to that firm and withdraw its inventory, without requiring wholesaler credit. A wholesaler's financing failure therefore cannot alone prevent a funded household from buying locally available food. The published district quote updates to the quantity-weighted price of actual household purchases where sales occur.

Food coverage is min(1, delivered food / subsistence food), separately for each stratum. Material coverage weights fulfillment of tools 35%, textiles 25%, timber 15%, stone 10%, coal 10% and iron 5%. Regional coverage is weighted by people. Mortality uses the household stratum's food shortage, so plentiful aggregate stocks cannot protect households unable to buy food. Wealth is a real-balance display index clamp(log(1+1,000 × cash/(person × local basket price index))/log(21),0,1); actual cash, not that index, constrains purchases.

## Input-output production and prices

Raw production is constrained by paid labor, geography and technology; agricultural labor has diminishing returns as specified under Production and carrying capacity. Manufacturing uses fixed input coefficients, a Leontief quantity constraint. Hiring assigns workshop labor only to production supported by opening inputs, sharing those inputs among local firms. Procurement retains the unconstrained output orders, so a stockout releases idle workshop labor without erasing the demand needed to restart it:

```text
Q capacity[j] = output supported by the allocated paid labor and productive capital
Q planned[j] = max(desired inventory-adjusted order, Q capacity[j])
Q actual[j] = min(Q capacity[j], available input[i] / recipe[i,j] for every input i,
                  affordable material quantity, available natural reserve where applicable)
input order target[i] = household reserve[i] + 2 × sum(recipe[i,j] × Q planned[j])
unit replacement cost = allocated wage bill / capacity units + input recipe cost
quote target = max(0.01, replacement cost × (1+markup))
firm price *= exp(clamp(log(target/current price), −0.06, 0.06))
wholesale order = max(0, household reserve + planned input reserve
                         + max(one month of household needs, wholesaler sales forecast) − market stock)
expected monthly sales S = max(1, household need, wholesaler sales forecast)
target stock T = household reserve + planned input reserve + export buffer
retail markup = 0.04 + (max(0,T−S) × spoilage rate + T × financing rate) / S
retail quote = (1 + retail markup) × weighted acquisition price, when purchases occur
```

Firms allocate labor by product. Surplus arms cannot, by itself, cancel food jobs. Benchmark output uses each firm's technology, geography, productivity and capital at its share of the local labor force; product labor requirements are then normalized. All hiring still needs wages and bank capacity. Manufacturing also needs cash to purchase inputs. Planned output is recorded before shortages reduce actual output, preserving material orders when an input runs out.

The wholesaler purchases the cheapest available firm offers first, with stable ID tie-breaking and no overdraft. Retail carrying costs use monthly spoilage rates of 3.5% for food, 0.6% for timber/textiles and 0.1% for other goods, and the principal-weighted rate on outstanding wholesaler loans. With no debt, the current policy rate plus the lending spread is the financing opportunity cost. The additional 4% is a trading margin. This avoids systematically pricing below the cost of holding the modeled reserve. Unsold firm inventories persist and spoil. Firm markups fall 0.002 when inventories exceed two forecast months and rise 0.001 otherwise, bounded between 4% and 30%. Firm price adjustment is limited to six log-percentage points per month, with a 0.01 nominal floor. An inactive market retains its quote. These are behavioral disequilibrium rules, not a solved competitive equilibrium; rationing and unemployment can persist.

Purchases between regions settle at the seller's quoted price. The buyer also pays any import tariff to its government and a freight fee to the origin laborer account. District freight fee per tonne is seller price × route effort × 0.002. Price, transport effort, domestic preference and relationship persistence rank supplier contacts. Buyer cash and remaining physical corridor capacity bound delivery. The freight service uses the existing abstract transport sector and does not separately hire carrier workers; its receipts enter mixed income and service output. Freight and tariffs enter the destination inventory acquisition value.

## Investment, profits and enterprise succession

```text
private capital after wear = capital × (1−0.0015)
shared capacity after wear = capacity × (1−0.0015) × (1−0.002 devastation)
desired new firm capacity = max(0, jobs / desired jobs − 0.75) × 0.03502
                            + capital × 0.0015
monthly real finance cost = (1 + monthly loan rate) × exp(−expected annual inflation / 12) − 1
user cost = real finance cost + 0.0015 depreciation
expected remaining retail = sum(price × min(stock, max(0, forecast − sold))) / 1000
operating yield = (sales + expected remaining retail − payroll − inputs)
                  / max(unit construction cost, capital × unit construction cost)
desired new capacity = 0 when operating yield ≤ user cost
construction inputs per unit = 300 t timber + 400 t stone + 40 t tools
construction labor per unit = 1,000 artisan person-months
actual construction = min(desired, input availability, unused artisan labor,
                          23.396% of firm cash / full material-and-labor cost)
```

Funded productive projects are considered before government hiring, household consumption and residential construction. Firms buy construction materials and pay actual artisan wages with ordinary withholding. Own-account construction is valued at those costs, recorded as fixed investment and queued for six months before productive use. Materials are intermediate consumption of the construction activity; the finished capital is output. This prevents counting materials twice in GDP. Public industrial construction also takes six months; existing other construction commands retain their paid immediate-development abstraction. Shared regional capacity supplements each firm's own capital, so operating firms retain their local land and infrastructure environment.

The tax/distribution base is cash sales less operating payroll, settled input purchases and interest. Only positive cash surplus is taxed. Firms distribute up to 45% of positive after-tax surplus, bounded by available deposits; wholesalers distribute 20% plus 2.564% per month of cash above a six-month observed operating-cost buffer and conservative construction-project reserve. This is a cash-basis tax rule, distinct from national-account accrual surplus. Capital expenditure is capitalized, and inventory holding gains are not taxed by this rule.

Firms unable to fund 5% of desired employment, or holding less than one payroll while making operating losses, accumulate distress. The distress score rises by one in each such month and falls by one, to a minimum of zero, in each recovery month. Reaching eighteen triggers liquidation. In districts with more than 500 residents, entry is considered annually at staggered district dates, or monthly when no active enterprise remains. Owners must hold twice the seed subscription, max(1, civilian population × 0.00015), and the active-firm count must be below the population-dependent target described under Local specialization. Entry transfers existing owner deposits, creates no free capital and retains a new generation number. Initial and entrant productivity differences use the world seed, never culture or ethnicity.

## National accounts and measurement

Three separately accumulated views must agree for every state and the whole world:

```text
GDP production = gross output − intermediate consumption + product taxes
GDP expenditure = household consumption + government consumption
                  + gross fixed investment + inventory transaction changes
                  + exports − imports
GDP income = paid gross wages + gross operating surplus + mixed income + product taxes
business accrual surplus = operating receipts − operating purchases − wages
                           + own-account capital output + inventory transaction changes
inventory holding gains = closing inventory value − opening value
                          − inventory transaction flows − other asset changes
```

The income account is built from each business's receipts, expenses, wages, construction and inventory movements; it is not assigned the difference required to make GDP balance. Every inventory entry and withdrawal has a quantity and transaction value. A separate closing physical snapshot detects missing movements. Sales from wholesalers contribute the selling margin above replacement cost, rather than the full value of resold goods. Firms' own output is valued at their contemporaneous quote. Withdrawals use the latest recorded acquisition/replacement values, carried across months until a new quote arrives. A retailer’s selling markup is not written into the cost of unsold stock; it becomes trade output on sale. Revaluation of existing stock is a holding gain, excluded from all three GDP measures.

Government inventory purchases are recorded when acquired, and military food/ammunition become government consumption when used. Government nonmarket output is measured by paid labor and used supplies. Materials incorporated into paid recruitment, repairs or construction are intermediate inputs to those services. Requisition, estates, liquidation gifts and observer shocks are explicit in-kind transfers or other asset changes; they are not newly produced goods. Normal spoilage is an operating inventory loss. Exceptional destruction is an other-volume change. Foreign grants funded on behalf of an importing wholesaler are recorded as purchases settled by a third party, with a capital transfer financing the recipient.

Exports and imports are recorded at the bilateral transaction value, including cross-border freight where applicable. Freight is self-employed output/mixed income; import tariffs are product taxes. Internal trade cancels in national consolidation and world exports equal world imports. Income/profit taxes, welfare, loan proceeds, principal, interest, dividends, bank bail-ins and financial write-downs are excluded from production expenditure. Territorial attribution is frozen at the beginning of each month, so a mid-month conquest does not move already-recorded output between countries.

```text
reference-price goods output = sum((physical output − industrial inputs) × initial prices)
reference-price public services = paid public labor × initial wage by skill
basket index = current subsistence basket cost / its cost at initial prices
unemployment = 1 − employed labor / participating labor
```

The reference-price output indicator is narrower than the reconciled nominal GDP measure: it does not deflate every service and margin or provide a chain-linked real GDP series. The basket index uses current needs weights; it is not a fixed-weight CPI. Grouped income Gini uses district-stratum per-person disposable income: 1 − sum(n[j] × (C[j−1]+C[j]))/(N×Y), for sorted groups, counts n, total people N, total income Y and cumulative income C. Zero aggregate income displays zero; inequality inside a pooled stratum is unobserved.

Reconciliation is enforced every month with scale-aware floating-point tolerances, separately from deposit, bank and physical-goods identities. No statistical discrepancy is used as a balancing plug. Full reconciliation applies to the sectors represented here; it does not imply complete SNA sector coverage or empirical predictive validity.

## Geography and cartography

Generator 3 uses separated continental nuclei with seeded domain warping and angular coastline variation, plus smaller offshore islands. An ocean frame keeps land away from the map boundary. Curved ridge fields have spatially varying strength, broken passes and separate upland blocks, creating uneven mountain belts. Latitude, elevation, moisture noise, and a simple dry-latitude/rain-shadow term choose biomes. This is synthetic terrain generation, not plate tectonics or an atmospheric simulation.

A priority flood removes drainage sinks by minimally raising their elevations. Each land cell stores a lower, adjacent downstream cell. Runoff accumulates in reverse drainage order; visible rivers begin at 12 runoff units and continue to the sea. Rivers increase local moisture and add a small contribution to provincial fertility. These runoff units are relative catchment weights, not measured cubic meters per second. River flow is geographic and does not yet change with monthly rainfall.

Each land cell has a geographic habitat score and a correlated settlement-attraction field. Wet lowlands, rivers and coasts are favored; mountains, tundra and deserts support much less settlement. These are geography-based synthetic starting conditions, unrelated to ethnicity or culture.

```text
habitat = clamp(terrain suitability × (0.65 + 0.5 moisture) + 0.15 river)
terrain suitability = 0.90 plains, 0.64 forest, 0.38 hills,
                      0.035 mountains, 0.045 desert, 0.025 tundra
attraction = clamp(habitat × (0.12 + 0.88 basin_noise²) + 0.12 coast + 0.10 river)
settlement weight = habitat × (0.5 + 4 attraction²)
initial cell population weight = settlement weight
world initial civilian endowment ≈ 3,000 × land cell count
initial province population = world endowment × province weight / world weight
province seed priority = distance_to_nearest_seed² × (0.04 + 4 attraction³) × seeded jitter
```

Density-weighted seeds make prosperous basins more finely divided and inhospitable expanses coarser. Provinces grow with positive terrain costs, preserving connected land ownership. Their administrative anchors favor accessible settlement locations near their centroids; an anchor is not automatically a town. Capitals favor populated regions on substantial landmasses, with geographic separation. State expansion follows the province graph; unclaimed offshore provinces join the closest capital. None of these rules constrain later military conquests. Initial urbanization, infrastructure, literacy and industrial capital follow the population-weighted attraction score rather than independent uniform draws. The population prior includes a rural base so that attraction does not put most people into tiny provinces beyond their starting food-supply capacity. Before capital-city overrides, industrial and infrastructure indices are rescaled (with a 1.2 ceiling) to population-weighted means of 0.47 and 0.50. This redistributes the opening endowment while keeping its aggregate scale comparable to the preceding generator; it is not new investment or a payment during simulation. Rounding the three initial age totals can change a province's endowment by one person; subsequent cohort accounting is exact. Initial military personnel are drawn from these civilians.

Resources are sampled across every land cell. Their potential is area-averaged into the opening provinces and then each active district. Forest cover governs timber; uplands concentrate stone. Iron and coal occur in separate smooth, thresholded fields:

```text
raw iron = max(0, noise(9x,9y) − 0.58)² × (0.4 + 2 elevation)
raw coal = max(0, noise(6x,6y) − 0.57)² × max(0.1, 1 − elevation)
resource potential = raw field × target world mean / actual world mean
```

Coordinates x,y are normalized to the map extent; fields use distinct saved-seed salts. World-average potential is normalized to 0.80 for timber, 0.45 for stone, 0.35 for iron and 0.45 for coal when the raw field is nonzero. Normalization leaves all zero deposits at zero. These are synthetic resource basins, not geological survey data. When district simulation initializes, each cell receives natural reserves of 150,000 tonnes times its stone, iron or coal potential, and 5,000 tonnes times its timber potential. These are unextracted endowments, separate from owned goods inventories. Mineral extraction depletes them; local extraction potential falls in proportion to remaining reserves. Standing timber regrows by 0.2% of the gap to its land-use-adjusted capacity per month; built land reduces that capacity by up to 80%. Splitting a district cannot reset these cell-level stocks.

### Current adaptive space, sites and administration

The first monthly step initializes `adaptive.version = 1`. Physical sites, legal owners, computational leaves and jurisdictions have independent identities. A site retains its physical location and household/merchant/firm reference. The quadtree clips existing terrain polygons in meter coordinates, retains disconnected fragments, uses stable string addresses, and enforces a 2:1 neighboring-width ratio. Existing land, drainage and natural reserves are retained. Refinement does not discover new ore or create buildings.

Every six months, resident/job activity, live-site workload, known terrain variation, coastlines, river crossings, corridor traffic, fleets and price heterogeneity select resolution with split/merge thresholds 1/0.55 and a twelve-month dwell period. The minimum width is 128 m. A separate live-site term (site count / 64) refines densely occupied areas even when geographic error is small. Fixed coarse and fine experiments use 16 km and 1 km target widths. Camera position and machine speed do not enter these decisions. Land and water share the same dynamic tree; exact clipped faces distinguish land connections from water gaps. Recursive range and nearest-site queries serve housing, work and inspection. See [Adaptive grid](ADAPTIVE_GRID.md) for topology and source-data limits. Extensive node summaries add populations, jobs and clipped land area; prices retain their underlying observations rather than being summed.

Local markets and household pools remain exact heterogeneous closure records. Firm identities, inventory owners, bank accounts, ownership shares and loan contracts do not move to newly numbered numerical cells. All production, procurement and household calculations retain those underlying records at every resolution. This gives exact partition independence, but **does not yet reduce the cost of nonlinear firm/cohort calculations by replacing them with aggregate agents**. Coarse–fine interfaces are explicit geometric records; actual transfers use the existing shared physical transport network and transaction ledger once, independent of leaf boundaries.

Municipalities and provinces are dated jurisdictions over this landscape. Annual local search evaluates neighboring mergers, connected splits, and one-cell catchment transfers. It values four annual return trips to a service seat at one quarter of the hourly wage, plus office staffing and increasing workload. Thirty-six months of expected savings must repay actual reorganization wages. Several disjoint beneficial reforms can proceed in each annual review; every proposal rechecks available labor and treasury. Merged administrations retain local service offices, each with staffing overhead, so consolidation does not force all residents to visit one distant seat. Reforms require available labor, spendable treasury funds and stability of at least 25. Geometry is validated before any mutation: complete non-overlapping coverage, a seat within each component, connectivity and unchanged sovereignty. A funded reform records wages/public output; merely changing a polygon contributes no GDP.

Predecessors become tombstones with successor IDs and dated cell assignments. Past accounts and contracts retain their recorded attribution. Public accounts are currently national, so municipal/provincial changes do not divide or novate sovereign debt. Separate conquest or sovereignty events cause a recorded jurisdiction reconciliation, preserving predecessor claims. Islands can share a jurisdiction only through an existing sea connection. Candidate legal boundaries currently follow original terrain polygons; they may cross several much finer computational leaves.

Compatibility references in armies, fleets, capitals, treaties, charters, historical effects, district censuses and market routes continue to address the original permanent geographic handles. `world.regions` is therefore also a statistical/legacy-reference layer, **not a list of the current dynamic provinces**. The map draws current administrative borders; local inspection and the area dossier identify overlapping current jurisdictions. This avoids changing an old contract's meaning when a province changes shape.

### Housing, work and freight

Opening inhabited terrain cells supply persistent residential sites; these are housing endowments, not automatically towns. Each site has an exact integer occupancy and physical footprint. Funded development can add several physical parcels inside one original terrain square. Firms have establishment sites and local merchants have market sites. Opening housing capacity is max(ceil(1.15 × residents), opening built fraction × 12,000). Opening establishment capacity derives from existing capital and labor plans. These are declared initial conditions, not construction output. Existing built cover is preserved.

Housing investors compare nearby infill with opening another serviced plot, using current input prices and wages. The base inputs per place are 0.08 tonnes of timber, 0.05 tonnes of stone and 0.1 worker-month, multiplied by an integrated convex density cost; new land additionally pays fixed servicing and distance-dependent access works. These real inputs discourage tiny repeated outward expansions without imposing a city-count limit. Demand includes an aggregate housing buffer and anticipated moves toward better job/service access, net of vacancies and pending projects. Funding, material stocks and unemployed labor constrain each project, which completes after three months. Numerical remeshing cannot create capacity. Opening rural records represent dispersed housing; substantial paid additions can raise their developed cover above the same 0.03 threshold as other occupied development. See [the construction equations](MATHEMATICS.md#housing-commuting-and-daily-inspection) for coefficients and their synthetic status. Materials leave owned inventories, wages transfer to real workers, and completed capital enters the stock-flow accounts once.

After existing local enterprise contracts, remaining workers search accessible domestic workplaces. Vacancy and gross wage enter the weight multiplicatively, with exp(−minutes/30) travel disutility; 35% of available workers search in this monthly round. Simultaneous vacancy and cash constraints scale offers before wages settle. The present search neighborhood is 6 km with a 90-minute journey budget. Local retained employment uses a ten-minute within-market approximation. Walking is 4 km/h; established corridors and infrastructure above 0.5 permit rail at logistics level 3 and road motor travel at level 5. Terrain, devastation and corridor traffic raise generalized travel time. These search/mode coefficients are synthetic and require future sensitivity/calibration work.

Compact origin–firm rows retain five exact stratum FTE counts and five wage amounts, with shared physical route records. Enterprise payroll and job totals reconcile. Household residence receives wages and pays income tax; workplace production is counted once. Cross-border commuting is not inferred. Public and construction employment remain separately recorded in their existing paid service accounts.

Freight settles ownership and payment once. Shipments whose route takes more than 30 days remain explicitly owned inventory in transit and become available only at arrival. Transit inventory appears in both goods and national accounts; arrival transfers the same stock to the buyer's store exactly once. Shorter journeys remain within the monthly clearing approximation.

### Inspection and rendering

Growing settlements use the same varied market, planned and riverside town plans as the opening atlas. Seeded street traditions favor winding market lanes, with some spine settlements and more ordered districts; layouts are stable when population and capacity change. Larger illustrative houses, open squares, gardens, planted gaps, varied roofs and visible doors/windows replace the previous dense array of tiny blocks. The physical parcel still bounds the layout and buildings avoid water. Capacity affects bounded massing (two to six illustrated storeys), not unlimited vertical stacking into the renderer's finite lot budget. Visible heights use bounded cartographic proportions relative to house footprints; they are not literal architectural measurements. The sum of represented capacity metadata still equals the parcel's housing capacity. Distant views sample the same buildings as close-ups. Foundations now use small physical offsets instead of oversized map units. Detailed native scenery remains bounded to sixteen visible and twenty-four cached patches; legacy scenes retain six/twelve limits. These are visual budgets, not population limits. Observation does not build housing, change capacity or move residents.

Local daily-life inspection requests meter bounds and returns a versioned snapshot: full-area population/jobs/capacity totals, bounded site geometry, active jurisdiction names, and representative routines. Camera requests are debounced and stale replies cannot replace a newer selection. A snapshot contains at most 24 sampled homes and six routine groups per home, plus their required destinations. Zooming or changing the day clock consumes no economic random numbers and cannot pay wages or consume goods again. Paid workplace examples follow recorded route geometry. The day remains tied to its recorded month; refreshing explicitly selects the current month. These are conditional representative residents, not persistent causal individuals (phase 8).

### Legacy resident initialization and remapping

The following remapping rules describe the retained pre-adaptive conversion path. Once `adaptive` exists, later mesh changes use permanent closure records as described above; they no longer execute this financial/census repartitioning.

Every active quadtree leaf is clipped into connected land within one administrative province. Each resulting district is an actual simulation unit: it has dated age/sex/identity/stratum cohorts, household deposits, a merchant balance sheet, local goods stocks and quotes, infrastructure, soil fertility, natural resource access, resident firms and capital projects. Households consume only offers in their district. Firms hire locally, pay actual wages and buy locally available inputs. Shortages and prices can differ across two districts in the same province. Trade is required to bring goods across their boundary.

```text
pressure(node) = sum(city residents / 8,000 + industrial employment / 2,500)
industrial employment = firm employment × shares in tools, textiles, arms, iron and coal
split when pressure > 1 and span > one terrain cell
retain an existing split while pressure > 0.55; otherwise merge
```

The terrain grid is the minimum spatial resolution; refinement does not create new land. Conservation at remeshing does not establish resolution-independent economic outcomes; a formal mesh-convergence study remains outstanding. Proposals use actual cell residents and firm locations. Reorganization happens before opening a new monthly ledger, independently of zoom. Every province's administrative depot retains its identity and location. Political borders still determine jurisdiction; a city can extend across several provinces without annexing them.

The initial location prior retains dispersed rural residents and concentrated settlements. Concentrated population is round(province population × urban share). New centers require 1,200 × max(1, √(province cell count)/2) concentrated residents; opening sites are spaced at least three grid coordinates apart, with rank weights (rank + 1)^−1.15 and an initial maximum of eight per province. These limits apply only to initialization. Rural weights are habitat², excluding habitat below 0.06 when alternatives exist. The subsequent city system has no eight-center limit.

Boundary changes use a finite-volume remap over intersecting old and new cell sets:

```text
N[old → new] = sum(existing residents in cells belonging to both units)
sum(new district people) = sum(old district people)
Q[new,g] = sum(old goods allocated over each intersection)
K[new] = sum(allocated shared equipment and unfinished projects)
carrying price[new,g] = sum(quantity transferred × old carrying price) / new quantity
```

Opening stratum proportions are allocated first; joint identity groups occupy compact portions rather than being replicated across every destination. Each birth-month/sex bin is integer-apportioned with recipient census totals as hard capacities. No birthday, person, identity or economic stratum is invented or rounded away. Household deposits follow the actual relocated stratum counts; merchant deposits and inventories follow resident shares, with area shares for an empty source. The final recipient receives each floating-point stock residual. Cash moves through the existing transaction ledger. Household and merchant loan portions retain their original contract, lender, rate, origination date and arrears. Portions of the same original claim reunite on merger; unrelated contracts remain distinct. Bank and firm ownership shares follow their household beneficiaries. Shared equipment and project costs are additive. At the next monthly opening, a retired district with exactly zero cash, equipment, projects and outstanding claims becomes a compact lineage record; nonzero balances are never discarded. Finite mineral reserves remain attached to their physical cells.

Firms retain their IDs, cash, inventory, debt, equipment, projects and geographic sites. Only their containing district changes. Opening enterprises and later entries must be financed from existing merchant or owner funds. A boundary change itself produces no goods, writes off no loan, creates no deposit and contributes no GDP. Inventory relocation is a matched asset transfer at carrying value, so remeshing cannot manufacture holding gains.

The authoritative civilian census is serialized once, as province communities tagged with their resident district. District views reference those groups. Province statistics aggregate them; governments, recruitment and sovereignty use the administrative layer. Provincial material orders draw from the **physical administrative depot**, not from a frictionless sum of distant inventories. Other local markets can supply that depot only by paid freight. Public services and welfare are funded across the actual local households. Province-wide road, soil and damage interventions pass through to local conditions. Save validation rejects inconsistent district ownership, population, stocks, natural reserves, sites and financial records.

### Specialization and legacy built-footprint records

District labor plans respond to local demand and available inputs. The baseline occupational allocation is reweighted by soil suitability, local extraction potential and urbanization, normalized to sum to one. An agricultural establishment gives its food weight a factor of three; a mining establishment applies that factor to extraction, and an industrial establishment applies it to manufactures. All enterprises still adjust product-level hiring to observed sales and unfilled household/input requirements; specialization is not a permission to produce without workers or materials.

Legacy equipment indices are treated as extensive stocks. Local manufacturing uses equipment intensity, opening province population divided by current district population (denominator at least 500), multiplied by allocated equipment. This prevents a numerical subdivision alone from dividing the equipment available per worker. Manufacturing also receives a bounded external-economy multiplier:

```text
agglomeration = min(1.4, 1 + 0.04 × log(1 + district people / cells / 1,000))
entry target = min(24, max(2, ceil(sqrt(district population / 15,000))))
```

In districts with more than 500 residents, entry is evaluated annually at staggered district dates, or monthly when no active firm remains, and requires the owner to hold at least twice the required subscription. Subscription itself transfers money to the enterprise. Construction remains funded, material-constrained and delayed. Industrial and mining districts are classified from actual sector employment; agricultural districts reflect food employment and soil suitability. This is an agent-based spatial model with synthetic coefficients, **not** a solved or calibrated Krugman general equilibrium. The importance of market access, transport costs and increasing returns is motivated by [Krugman's economic-geography model](https://www.nber.org/papers/w3275).

The settlement catalogue records occupied morphology separately from municipalities. A connected developed footprint needs at least 500 residents to appear as a village, 5,000 for a town and 50,000 for a city. These are display classifications, never population targets. Active parcels need at least 300 residents/km² and funded development or physical developed cover of 0.03, independent of previous city labels. Parcels connect across gaps up to 150 m along their closest physical edges when the intervening land is traversable within the same state. The source-cell center is not a compulsory waypoint; water gaps and diagonal corner contacts without an accessible land passage do not merge cities. Empty parcels cannot bridge towns. These synthetic geometric rules are inspired by, but are not equivalent to, the [Degree of Urbanisation](https://human-settlement.emergency.copernicus.eu/degurbaDefinitions.php), which classifies population grids rather than these housing supports.

A city can contain many parcels across old terrain cells and administrative boundaries; two independent settlements can also occupy the same terrain square. Growth, occupied infill, depopulation and separation change membership. No city-count or maximum-population target is imposed. Rural and agricultural activity does not need an urban label. The old free built-growth rule is disabled in adaptive worlds: capacity is an opening endowment or funded construction. Landlords, land rents, mortgages and a market-clearing urban land price remain unimplemented.

Physical urban shares used by production are measured directly from occupied developed parcels. Fertility opportunity costs additionally use the size of connected occupied physical footprints, as described under demographics. City names, IDs, catalogue membership, ancestry and display classifications are never inputs to those calculations or the influence field. The previous drift toward an industry-based urbanization target is disabled in adaptive worlds. Renaming, removing or regrouping the city catalogue cannot change housing choice, capacity, money or population.

Households respond to overlapping economic and administrative influence spheres: reachable nonfarm jobs and funded municipal service offices, decayed by journey time. Municipal services combine paid administration, education and policing, expressed in base service-worker equivalents (real output / 0.0015), apportioned over the municipality's retained service seats. Neither an unfunded title nor a capital flag creates attraction. Both fields extend across household-pool and municipal boundaries within accessible domestic land. The field uses a 6 km source radius, a 60-minute journey limit, and the transport network with terrain, congestion and sovereign barriers. Within and between adjacent accessible terrain squares, conservative direct walking competes with the network route. Housing decisions use diminishing field gains and capacity crowding; actual moves still require exact traversable routes and vacancies. See [the equations](MATHEMATICS.md#settlement-formation-and-residential-choice).

The source field uses per-terrain-cell quadtrees, separate from the adaptive integration mesh. Coincident establishments aggregate exactly; distant branches are accepted only with a 2% relative bound on their positive exponential-kernel contribution and never across a travel/radius cutoff. Political or water barriers cannot be averaged away because path access is established before source aggregation. This is a controlled numerical approximation of the stated synthetic influence kernel, not an approximation of financial accounts or a calibration claim.

The browser simulation worker dispatches immutable shared numeric snapshots to up to 32 spatial workers (hardware concurrency minus two, further limited by workload). Batches contain at most 64 homes and use a shared work queue, so one dense urban terrain cell can occupy multiple workers. Each target's accumulation order is fixed. Population, housing, ledger and political mutations remain serial and conservative. A timed-out or failed field calculation is discarded and recomputed serially; unsupported browsers use the same serial kernel. Shared-memory headers are supplied by both local dev and preview servers. Field parallelism does not imply parallelization of the entire economic engine.

City IDs, founding dates, parent centers and aliases persist. Coalescing centers retain the older center's identity (then larger population and ID break ties); absorbed centers remain in the record. Comparable centers can acquire a compound name after at least five years since the retained name last changed. If the original nucleus leaves the footprint, the label moves to the most populated surviving physical site. An empty footprint retains its historical record. Newly formed centers receive deterministic geographic names. District names reflect their city or economic use. These mechanisms evolve toponyms without renaming whole populations or cultures.

Migration still uses simultaneous household plans, local earnings, employment, food access and social affinity. District neighbors, observed sea shipments and accessible vacant housing found by the adaptive tree supply destinations. Local choices subtract crowding, 0.04 × log(1 + population/(cells × 12,000)), and a travel penalty of grid distance × 0.002/(1 + destination infrastructure). Movers carry their deposits, proportional debts and ownership shares; their birth cohorts move exactly and nobody moves twice in one month. Adaptive worlds additionally use the commuting and funded residential construction described above; the old free built-growth rule is disabled. Legacy footprints describe built settlements, not OECD functional urban areas; those also include commuting zones, as explained by the [OECD methodology](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/oecd-definition-of-cities-and-functional-urban-areas.html).

The recursive hierarchy follows [Samet's quadtree survey](https://cs.umd.edu/~hjs/pubs/SameCSUR84-ocr.pdf). Quadtree pressure thresholds, geographic units, natural reserve sizes, agglomeration strength, travel penalties and land-use rates are synthetic assumptions requiring sensitivity testing and calibration. Conservation establishes accounting consistency, not historical realism or numerical convergence across resolutions.

Rendering extracts shared polygon topology from the hex grid, applies bounded shared-vertex offsets, and smooths the land silhouette. Interpolated terrain and slope lighting are cached independently of economic updates. Borders and quantitative fills read current simulation state. City and state labels use screen-space collision checks; ocean labels use open-water clearance. Hit testing follows province polygons, and wheel zoom preserves the geographic location under the pointer. The smoothed shore is a small visual approximation of the underlying boundary; it does not change simulation adjacency.

The version-1 save envelope remains supported. New saves carry `generatorVersion: 3`, optional per-cell landscape and drainage fields, and the adaptive spatial state; older exports without those fields remain valid and keep their saved terrain, people and resources. Their map derives settlement detail without mutating the save. The first monthly step initializes resident district accounts conservatively from their saved provincial state; no new money or people are awarded during conversion. Existing provincial terrain and administrative identities remain intact. Use New world to obtain the new physical resource and population distribution. New generation is reproducible within this generator version. Changed neighbor traversal and geography may produce different future histories than an older application build; same-build core continuation remains deterministic when supplied the same external historical inputs.

## Monthly order

1. Initialize permanent local accounts and adaptive sites if absent. Advance dated age cohorts; start money, goods and population ledgers; deliver previously purchased freight that has arrived.
2. Apply climate, historical effects, political pressure, leadership changes and diplomatic obligations.
3. Depreciate capital, complete construction, update sales expectations, hire and pay local workers, match funded domestic commuters, withhold income tax, produce goods and quote replacement-cost prices.
4. Operate fleets and clear paid, capacity-limited domestic and international shipments.
5. Fund welfare and public-service employment; households purchase goods; remaining inventories spoil.
6. Operate institutions, intelligence and security. Update health, education, births, deaths, retirement, migration, occupational mobility and identity diffusion.
7. Accumulate research; update diplomacy and autonomous decisions; supply, move, reinforce and fight with military formations.
8. Reconcile jurisdiction membership after sovereign changes, evaluate funded administrative reforms, and advance residential construction. Settle interest and principal, recognize credit losses, levy profit taxes, distribute dividends, commission affordable capital projects, resolve distressed enterprises and update the policy rate.
9. Reconcile money, bank balance sheets, physical goods and all three GDP measures. Update built cover, geographic footprints and names; aggregate local/statistical/national totals, refresh establishment sites and the camera-independent mesh, and record history.

Changing a policy affects subsequent ticks. Building a provincial improvement and sandbox interventions change state immediately and influence the next tick. A march order records a destination; movement occurs on subsequent ticks.

## Population accounting

The core invariant is:

```text
people after tick = people before tick + births - deaths
people = civilian children + civilian adults + civilian elders + living soldiers + naval crews
```

Recruitment transfers adults into an army. Migration transfers exact age cohorts between districts, including across provincial borders. Surrendered or disbanded troops return to civilian life. Military and civilian deaths both enter the world death ledger. Territory transfers and independence do not create people.

Each province contains joint population communities, rather than independent identity percentages. A community has age cohorts, cultural belonging, mother tongue, belief, political outlook and preferred civic allegiance. Culture and language registries begin from geographic communities that cross political borders. No culture or belief receives innate fertility, productivity or military coefficients. States have separate institutional identities; recognizing one changes policy, without relabeling residents.

Age is measured from each cohort's absolute birth month. A cohort contains female and male counts; children become adults at month 216 and adults become elders at month 780. Recruitment, migration, identity changes and demobilization preserve these exact birth dates and sex counts. Legacy saves distribute their recorded broad age totals deterministically across single-year ages, with a declining elderly distribution; this preserves the census without inventing a known past. New births create a cohort at the actual current month.

Mortality uses monthly survival probabilities derived from annual age-specific hazards. Fertility uses women aged 15–49 in seven five-year bands. Health, shortages, literacy, urbanization and historical effects modify outcomes. Seeded stochastic rounding preserves expected counts without requiring whole populations of individual agents. The exact equations and synthetic coefficients appear below.

Migration balances safety, food, infrastructure, health, contentment, diaspora connections, exclusion, cultural prestige and historical migration appeal against rootedness and border friction. Destinations are adjacent districts or endpoints of actual sea trade. A passenger-capacity proxy permits at most 0.15 people per tonne of delivered maritime freight in each direction; it is not a separately simulated passenger fleet or a shared freight/passenger capacity ledger. Closed borders suppress ordinary international movement, while a small refugee leakage remains. Domestic migration ignores national border policy. Severe devastation, hunger and exclusion can displace people. All departures are subtracted before arrivals, so newly arrived people cannot move again in the same month. Age, identity and a proportional share of the source stratum’s cash travel with migrants; expected employment and food-deflated wages contribute to destination appeal; displacement and old allegiances persist and gradually adjust.

### Identity formation and diffusion

Local contact, neighboring provinces and delivered trade expose communities to cultures, beliefs and political movements. Literature seeds small reader circles; belief events seed small follower communities. Novelty decays, and adoption is gradual. Government recognition alone creates no followers. Political outlook responds to education, exclusion, local attachment, commerce and institutions. Identities have names, origins, formation dates and parent lineages.

Shared state-language fluency can increase without changing mother tongue. Intergenerational language replacement is slower, with stronger common-language schooling effects; related dialects can also diffuse. The model tracks a mother tongue and fluency in one shared state language, not a complete multilingual repertoire. Cultural adoption is slower still. Sustained peaceful mixing may eventually form a shared local culture; long isolation and local schooling can produce a related dialect. New communities initially coexist with their parents. Integer thresholds mean very small adoption flows can take time to become visible.

Civic, pluralist and heritage citizenship; local, multilingual and common-language schools; and protected, favored or restricted belief policies determine access and pressure. Exclusion generates grievance, weakens loyalty and encourages movement. Conquest preserves community identities and old political allegiances. Civic allegiance can gradually shift toward the host state with confidence in it, or toward local autonomy under sustained grievance. Recruitment draws from adults with loyalty-weighted participation, and unrest uses actual social tension instead of a fixed foreign-culture penalty. State agents and historical generation receive census composition and social indicators.

The Population and province inspectors expose identity distributions, community conditions and monthly flows. Culture, language and belief map layers show the largest community per province; provinces remain internally mixed. Save validation requires community ages to sum exactly to each province census and troop profiles to sum to army strength. When allegiance changes make two joint identity keys equal, canonicalization sums their integer age counts and averages social conditions by people, retaining all individuals. Restore/autosave may recover an equivalent duplicate representation only after every profile and the declared census totals pass validation. Disk checksums are verified against original bytes before this in-memory recovery; the source checkpoint is preserved. Legacy saves receive a deterministic geographic census without adding or removing people; this initializes a plausible composition rather than reconstructing an unrecorded past.

These remain aggregate communities, not individual biographies or families. Household accounts are pooled by district and economic stratum. Marriage, explicit family descent, within-stratum asset inequality and empirically calibrated fertility schedules are not simulated.

## State character and leadership

Every state has six continuous tendencies: assertiveness, openness to reform, solidarity, centralization, commercial orientation and patience. Each combines three separate sources: the people's attitudes, institutional habits, and the current head of state or governing body's temperament. Cultural or religious identity names confer no innate personality.

Public attitudes are weighted from civilian and military adults and elders, using political movements, literacy, urbanization, wealth, grievance, loyalty, rootedness, displacement, real trade activity and war exhaustion. They adjust 2.5% toward current circumstances each month. Institutional habits move 0.8% monthly toward assumptions associated with the form of government. These assumptions are explicit synthetic model choices, not calibrated claims about real governments:

| Government family           | People | Institutions | Leader |
| --------------------------- | -----: | -----------: | -----: |
| Republic                    |    48% |          29% |    23% |
| Federation or confederation |    50% |          32% |    18% |
| Commonwealth or assembly    |    55% |          25% |    20% |
| Monarchy                    |    23% |          30% |    47% |
| Empire or autocracy         |    16% |          30% |    54% |
| Unclassified form           |    35% |          35% |    30% |

Effective state direction moves 12% monthly toward a conviction-adjusted weighted combination. The political-extremity setting amplifies distance from the midpoint, moderated by institutional/public/leader influence and personal dogmatism; it does not erase those constraints. The inspector shows its strongest tendencies, current priorities, sources of influence and detailed comparisons. A reform-minded public and a traditional ruler can coexist. The distance between public attitudes and leadership contributes a small social-cohesion cost.

Leaders also have a persistent private political profile with eight drives: ambition, risk tolerance, dominance, opportunism, vengefulness, dogmatism, empathy and vanity. Ten seeded archetypes include territorial hegemons, revanchists, predatory oligarchs, revolutionary reformers, doctrinal guardians, mercantile opportunists, encirclement obsessives, glory-seeking adventurers, constitutional stewards and civic solidarists. Each has an ambition, a red line and a blind spot. Motives correlate with temperament without predetermining every action; a public-interest leader can be confrontational and an ambitious leader can face strong institutional limits. Existing leaders gain motives without replacing their names, skills, perks or recorded temperaments. Extreme dispositions remain tendencies, not mechanical guarantees of war.

Leaders have persistent temperaments and five skills: administration, diplomacy, logistics, scholarship and command. Two strengths and one limitation are assigned deterministically from the world seed, state, tenure, succession generation and recorded name. Examples include commercial contacts, scholarly patronage, methodical administration, command reputation, impulsiveness, patronage dependence and distrust. These affect decision preferences and modest institutional effectiveness, scaled by executive influence. Their combined contribution to each supported effectiveness channel is bounded to ±9%; historical effects retain their existing combined 0.2–2.5 multiplier bounds. Transport, tax collection, funded education/research, command effectiveness and morale use these contributions. Resources, treaty consent and ownership constraints remain enforced separately.

New worlds begin with character-dependent policy allocations and research priorities. Older saves acquire profiles without changing their existing policies or recorded leaders. Private motives affect native military and conscription targets, autonomous responses and research through temperament, and enter state-agent strategic assessments. Strategic context includes actual supplied force ratios, neighboring states, resource-production asymmetries, material constraints and active disputes. Agents are explicitly allowed to pursue selfish, unequal or risky initiatives, and must consider a bold attainable alternative to accommodation or waiting. Referee plausibility does not require prudence or benevolence; risky undertakings still cannot fabricate assets, victories or consent. State-agent and historical-generation prompts receive the evolving profile; other states see public posture and leadership rather than private motives or skill records. The native government fallback uses the same tendencies for policy adjustment, trade/alliance propensity, war risk, exhaustion tolerance and research focus. The legacy four-value `personality` field remains only for version-1 compatibility, not as the behavioral driver.

Succession assigns a fresh leader profile and immediately moves executive direction 45% toward the new weighted target, retaining institutional and popular attitudes. It triggers a strategic review, preserving inherited state memory, signed agreements, population and resources. Replies from an agent deliberating under the old leader are discarded without misconduct penalties. Leader traits cannot be rewritten through ordinary agent orders or unsupported claims. Individual lifespans, family trees, elections, candidate selection and constitutional procedure are not simulated; leadership turnover still enters through historical events. Behavioral consistency of live LLM agents awaits the consolidated validation pass.

## Structural instability and observer controls

Simulation conditions are saved per world and can be changed through **Simulation conditions** or selected at world creation: instability 0–3 (default 1), raw-resource abundance 0.35–1.6 (default 0.85), and political extremity 0–2 (default 1.2). Changes preserve stocks, people, leaders and treaties. Extremity changes the strength of convictions immediately through subsequent monthly adjustment and shapes newly generated leaders; existing ambitions persist.

A separate saved PRNG generates additional disturbances independently of model requests. Instability targets roughly `2 × intensity` disturbances per simulated year world-wide, with exponential arrival times calibrated for integer months, a maximum one-month arrival rate and a 120-month upper interval. Zero suppresses new additional disturbances; existing effects, ordinary climate and endogenous shortages/politics continue. The four scopes are province (20%), state (18%), connected geographic region (29%) and international dispute (33%). State pressures currently involve competing factions. Local/regional cases also include harvest failures, extraction interruptions and epidemics. Regional effects follow contiguous neighboring provinces and may cross borders.

International pressures create concrete competing frontier claims, resource-concession disputes or contested transit rights at actual neighboring provinces. They reduce relations, accumulate grievances and may increase transit friction; they never declare a war, transfer territory, invent agreement signatures or create resources. The public ledger records up to 64 recent pressures and feeds affected agents and contextual history generation. A new crisis permits strategic reconsideration after a one-minute strategic cooldown, still subject to the existing thirty-second state cooldown, five-second global launch spacing and concurrency limits. Effects resolve through the native historical mechanism, so pressure frequency does not increase the model request budget. Wider region records can contain up to two effects per province; ordinary model-authored historical records retain their twenty-effect limit.

## Production and carrying capacity

Potential labor comes from exact age-specific participation and health. Competing firms hire only workers whose wage bill they can fund from deposits or bank credit. Product-specific sales expectations and inventories determine desired employment; devastation reduces it. Public employment and construction use remaining labor, preventing double employment. Urbanization and production priority set benchmark sector shares; firms adapt their actual mix to each product’s planned demand, as specified under Strata, labor and wages.

Farm output has diminishing returns to labor and a land-based upper bound:

```text
arable capacity = district terrain-cell count × 1,800 × (0.35 + 0.65 × fertility)
effective farm labor = capacity × (1 - exp(-farm workers / capacity))
equipment multiplier = 1 + 0.5 × log(1 + effective productive capital)
food output = effective farm labor × (0.105 + 0.125 × fertility) × abundance × equipment multiplier × technology × season × climate × efficiency
```

In model 7, existing productive capital also represents farm equipment, with the diminishing-return multiplier above. An unstaffed farm still produces nothing, and capital must be paid for through the existing six-month investment process. This prevents adding people from creating unlimited farmland. Agricultural investment and research increase supportable population. A firm's terrain cell determines its geographic season: the southern cycle is six months later than the northern cycle. Seasonal output varies by ±8% in tropical climates, ±12% in arid climates and ±23% elsewhere; the twelve-month mean multiplier is one. Annual world climate still varies from 0.82 to 1.16 of baseline. Temperature and biome use synthetic latitude, elevation and moisture rules, not a weather simulation. Industrial technology, literacy, and damage influence overall production efficiency.

Native cabinets review domestic policy every six months, including under Max integration. Merely having diplomacy records no longer disables their response to food shortages. Human governments remain in control; a model agent’s accepted plan defers the native fallback for six months, after which it can resume if the model has stopped responding. A state below 97% food coverage prioritizes agriculture and, until level six, switches research to agriculture without discarding progress in other fields.

Hungry native states also review farm improvements quarterly. A review can spend up to 2% of existing treasury per month, compounded over the review interval (5.88% at three months, 11.42% at six). It funds one feasible local project, trying the greatest population-weighted shortages first. There is no automatic borrowing or fixed nominal 450k charge. Each full terrain-cell improvement uses 5 t timber, 7 t stone and 100 construction person-months, priced at actual local material quotes and occupational wages. Actual investment scales down for treasury, material, worker and soil limits; increments smaller than 1% of a project are deferred. It increases fertility by up to 0.12, capped at 1.2 including already funded work, after six months. Projects record their terrain cells, so their paid benefits follow land across district remapping and conquest. Materials are intermediates of own-account construction and wages are paid once; the total is fixed investment, not a resource grant. This remains a soil/drainage-work abstraction rather than an agronomic crop or irrigation-network model.

Manual and model-issued farm orders use this same local project mechanism, funding the first feasible district in the selected province. Completed works and already-funded projects do not depend on continued AI control. Farm improvement is considered after ordinary private hiring and trade, before other capital investment and public-service hiring; it uses remaining workers rather than removing people from an ongoing harvest.

Matched late-game results, maximum-abundance and monthly checks, and the remaining food-access limits are recorded in the [agricultural response validation](validation/agriculture-2026-10-28.md).

Timber, stone, iron, and coal throughput depends on allocated workers, resource abundance and the district's remaining resource richness. New worlds distribute iron and coal through separate spatially correlated geological fields with thresholds for workable deposits; some provinces have none, others have concentrated sources. Legacy provinces receive local natural endowments on conversion. Scarce industrial inputs constrain manufacturing and create reasons for access agreements, leverage and conflict. Local richness declines with remaining natural reserves. After extraction, standing timber recovers once per month: R[t+1] = R[after extraction] + max(0,K−R[after extraction]) × 0.002, with K = initial timber potential × 5,000 × (1−0.8 × built fraction). Built land reduces carrying stock; minerals do not regenerate. Forest age classes and ecological damage are not represented in that recovery rule. Industrial capacity constrains manufacturing. Recipe inputs are removed from stocks before outputs are credited:

| Output, 1 tonne | Inputs                                    |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Tools           | 0.65 t iron + 0.30 t coal + 0.25 t timber |
| Textiles        | 0.30 t timber + 0.12 t food               |
| Arms            | 0.90 t iron + 0.50 t coal + 0.30 t tools  |

These recipes are economic abstractions; timber in the textile recipe stands in for fibrous raw material. Factories can only produce the amount supported by the scarcest input. Military priority increases arms output; agricultural and industrial priorities alter labor allocation.

Food demand starts at 25 kg per adult-equivalent consumer per month, scaled by `0.95 + 0.15 × urbanization`. Children count as 0.65 adult equivalents, adults as 1, and elders as 0.85. The food requirement is divided among strata using their own age composition; other goods still use person counts. These are synthetic average energy-equivalence factors, not individual dietary prescriptions. Other household/maintenance requirements use `MONTHLY_NEED`, scaled by `0.65 + 1.3 × urbanization + 0.3 × infrastructure + 0.3 × industry`; iron/coal use one quarter of their base rate and household arms use 15%. Reserves cover 2.4 months of household food demand or three months of other household demand, plus two months of planned manufacturing recipe inputs, computed before input constraints. A stockout therefore does not erase the demand needed to restart a factory. This avoids treating all raw industrial demand as household consumption while still retaining inputs for workshops. Firm quotes adjust toward replacement cost plus a bounded inventory-responsive markup, as specified under Input-output production and prices. Adjustment has a bounded monthly rate and a strictly positive numerical floor; there is no hard nominal-price ceiling.

Households purchase a subsistence basket plus discretionary goods subject to cash. Food coverage and material coverage are calculated separately for each economic stratum and then population-weighted. Below the subsistence budget, food has priority. Other-good coverage uses the material weights listed under Household choice and deprivation. Coverage affects health, contentment and mortality; low material coverage erodes infrastructure.

Remaining food loses 3.5% monthly to storage/spoilage, timber and textiles 0.6%, and other stocks 0.1%. There is no arbitrary inventory cap. All spoilage is recorded separately from use. The goods identity includes firm and wholesale inventories, army stores and fleet stores; shipments are transfers rather than consumption.

## Goods routes

Desired reserves are replenishment targets, not export embargoes. A seller retains one month of local household and planned input use (reserve target divided by 2.4 for food, by 3 for other goods). The remainder is eligible for sale. Eligibility is captured before clearing, then reduced by actual exports: imported lots cannot be repeatedly resold within the same monthly pass. This allows neighboring food deficits to draw on precautionary stores while preventing a chain of bookkeeping resales from generating spurious trade margins. Producer forecasts include the wholesaler's actual sales to outside markets, so export demand can change local production.

Local market clearing runs on a district network. Neighboring districts provide candidate shortest paths over their actual adjacent land cells; the border itself supplies no road or free transport. Civilian markets select a sparse shared road backbone from those candidates and surveyed provincial trunks, rather than activating every neighboring connection. Surveyed provincial trunk roads and maritime corridors remain available as long-distance links, with explicit last-mile paths to the local markets. Sea corridors use connected water, physical harbors and naval passage rules. A land trunk cannot bypass a hostile border. Foreign purchases observe war closures, treaty preferences, shared institutional trade arrangements and import tariffs. Contractual public deliveries also consume goods, treasury funds and physical route capacity.

```text
land effort = average terrain difficulty + 12 × elevation difference + river-crossing penalty
terrain difficulty = plains 1, forest 1.5, hills 2, mountains 5, desert 1.8, tundra 2
local road capacity ∝ (1,000 + 6,000 infrastructure) × (1 + 0.25 logistics)
                     × (1 − 0.6 devastation) / sqrt(terrain effort)
freight cost per tonne = seller quote / 1,000 × route effort × 0.002
affordable tonnes = buyer deposits / (unit goods price × (1 + tariff) + unit freight)
shipped tonnes = min(order, exportable stocks, affordable tonnes,
                     every shared segment's remaining capacity / traversal count)
```

Local infrastructure and damage determine segment capacities. The same physical edge budget is shared by all goods, all competing paths and opposite directions. Repeated traversal consumes capacity repeatedly. Only actual paid transfers move inventories; no district receives a copy of another's stock. Freight is income to transport households, and tariffs settle to the importing government. Domestic and international goods, service margins, freight, inventories and taxes continue to reconcile through independent GDP accounts.

District merchants search by physical route effort up to 8 + 5 × infrastructure + 2 × logistics technology. The horizon is spatial, not a fixed count of neighboring districts; subdividing a city does not consume an arbitrary supplier-slot allowance. Affordable offers rank by delivered costs. Orders cover household reserves and planned manufacturing inputs. Food orders clear first, followed by other goods. Credit can finance inventory subject to the existing bank capital and borrower limits. Quotes adjust toward delivered acquisition cost with a 4% trading margin, bounded to a 6% monthly logarithmic change per shipment.

Corridors learn from actual traffic. Familiarity gains 0.1 × sqrt(current tonnage/base capacity), loses 2.5% per month when unused, and can reduce route effort by up to 70%. Capacity increases by up to 80% with familiarity. Smoothed traffic adjusts by 20% toward current tonnage; congestion adds up to eight times uncongested effort through 1.5 × (traffic/capacity)². Trunk planning retains its quarterly review and minimum twelve-month path tenure. Cost-only replanning requires a 5% improvement. All district shipments, including those inside a province, contribute to physical corridor learning. The province-route API is only a display and diplomatic summary; the district shipment log records actual endpoints and cell paths.

Nearby trunk paths also consolidate physically. Long, actually used connections seed shared trunks first, ordered by descending endpoint span with stable endpoint-ID tie breaks. Up to four cleanup passes then let each eligible leg temporarily release its own edges and find a better shared path, including reconsidering earlier trunks. The objective is occupied unreinforced corridor effort counted once per physical edge, plus 0.15 times cumulative current travel effort. Searches stay within eight traversable cells of the original path; alternative searches with more emphasis on travel can recover a bounded join when the strongest attraction proposes an excessive detour. Every accepted replacement improves the same marginal objective, shares at least three consecutive edges with another path, and adds at most sixteen edges, 25% unreinforced effort and 40% current learned/congested cost against its input path. The complete proposed network must also improve the total objective before adoption. Ports, selected harbor-water cells, navigable water and the leg's permitted provinces are preserved. Seeding requires smoothed freight on at least 80% of the leg's non-port edges; cleanup considers the complete surveyed network. Quarterly review and twelve-month tenure still control eligibility. These planning weights do not discount actual freight bills or create capacity; merged traffic consumes the same physical segment budget. Local district-neighbor paths continue to use their district-constrained shortest-path search.

Civilian road activation is separate from path surveying and nearby-path fusion. After border and capacity checks, both provincial and district trade select a deterministic spanning forest ranked by physical terrain effort. Domestic components connect first, so domestic access does not depend on a foreign shortcut. A small number of relief links keep journeys within three times the terrain effort available in the candidate land graph; this avoids continent-sized detours between nearby places. Other cross-links require both endpoints to have infrastructure at least 0.7, national logistics at least 3, and an incident segment with familiarity at least 0.65 carrying remembered traffic of at least 0.1. They must shorten an existing road journey whose effort exceeds 1.6 times the direct candidate. Each eligible place receives at most one extra cross-link at logistics 3, two at 5 and three at 7 or above. These are explicit network-development heuristics, not empirically calibrated construction costs or a calendar-age gate. Selection is derived during market clearing from current legal candidates and saved development/traffic; it introduces no separate save format. Surveyed geometry retains its existing quarterly/annual inertia, while active civilian connections may change with access, geography, development or traffic. Military terrain access keeps its existing rules. Actual shipments follow the selected roads and share their original costs and segment capacity; unused survey candidates are not drawn as freight.

Trade-port display memory is separate from current freight. Observed sea-leg endpoints retain their first-use, last-use and update dates plus smoothed monthly throughput. Active throughput adjusts with `1 − 0.8^elapsedMonths`; idle throughput has a 24-month half-life. Markers remain fully visible for 36 quiet months, fade linearly over the next 24, and are retired after 60. Current cargo, selected freight and ships still come only from actual routes. Persistence validates unique endpoints and dates; coarse steps use elapsed time rather than one update per tick.

Shipping receives its own shared physical backbone from the initial survey. Short port approaches connect first, then crossings join remaining water components; existing water junctions connect immediately without an intermediate port call. All original connection pairs, harbor cells, harbor-water approaches and land access legs remain available. Relief crossings limit each movable sea leg to twice its input sailing effort on the surveyed water graph. Developed ports use the same infrastructure/logistics/traffic gate as roads, with traffic evidenced on actual water approaches; useful extra crossings are added when the current sailing path exceeds 1.35 times the direct survey. The per-port allowance is one, two or three at logistics 3, 5 or 7 respectively. New worlds start consolidated. Existing saves retain their saved geometry until the quarterly review and twelve-month path tenure permit change; locked paths anchor the network. Replanning uses only existing navigable surveyed segments and caps temporary predecessor caching at eight port searches. Naval passage and actual shared capacity are evaluated on the adopted paths. These weights are network-development heuristics, not funded harbor/route construction or a multicommodity optimum.

Freight that fits within the monthly clearing interval is delivered in that interval. Longer modeled journeys enter an owned in-transit inventory with a dated arrival; inventory and GDP accounting include those goods until they arrive exactly once. There are no persistent individual cargo agents or globally optimized multicommodity allocation. Failed or saturated routes can leave demand unmet. A finite spatial search horizon and greedy clearing are explicit behavioral assumptions, not proof of optimal allocation. Military movement still uses the legacy geographical region graph and its own access/supply phase.

## Historical causation and generated events

When globally enabled, OpenRouter generates contextual historical drafts using `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash` by default. The server reads credentials from `.env`; keys never enter prompts, worker state, exports or browser bundles. Model work is asynchronous. Time continues through missing credentials, slow requests, validation failures and provider outages.

A separate saved historical PRNG samples context-weighted states, provinces and themes: succession, discovery, literature, belief, institutions, social change, commerce, disaster and diplomacy. Event intervals follow a bounded exponential draw of 2–18 months, with war and low stability increasing the hazard. A draft request can remain in flight while the local simulation advances. At the next historical moment, a suitable validated LLM draft is preferred; otherwise a locally composed contextual event occurs. The native repertoire contains 47 distinct mechanisms across the nine categories, with seeded intensities, durations, response alternatives and conditional aftermath. Local recent titles suppress immediate repetition. Material conditions weight selection and shape the account; effects are resolved by the same bounded historical system as model-authored events. Up to three prepared drafts wait in a saved queue. Conditions, current territorial control and intervening leadership changes are checked before use; unsuitable drafts are discarded or expire. Existing climate, fiscal, research and military mechanisms also continue recording their own events.

New clients request a bundle containing one bespoke draft and up to three portable mechanisms; older clients retain the original event response contract. Portable mechanisms contain short generic developments and supported gain/friction modifier names, never executable instructions or arbitrary property paths. Up to 64 are retained in the save and can be instantiated with future local contexts and new outcomes; their model provenance remains recorded. This amortizes creative generation across many later events. History launches require both ninety seconds since the last launch and twenty-four simulated months; native events do not wait for that budget.

Each LLM draft contains a title and two or three short sentences, concrete effects, optional autonomous government responses and up to two conditional follow-ups. The API requests a kind-aware JSON Schema, then the worker checks IDs, scopes, units and effect compatibility independently. Actor-relative national target `-1` is resolved to the actual requesting state; it cannot select another state. Invalid structured responses receive one repair pass. The original integration defect was reproduced with a syntactically valid provider response containing unresolved national targets and an unsupported modifier; those are now handled by explicit target binding, separate effect-kind schemas and repair feedback.

Four effect kinds share a saved lifecycle:

- **Modifiers** affect harvests, extraction, manufacturing, transport friction/capacity, taxation, research, education, healthcare, births, mortality, migration appeal, social cohesion, military effectiveness and combat morale. World, state and province contributions compose; multiplicative channels clamp the combined factor to 0.2–2.5. Province contributions to national channels are weighted by population. Cohesion and migration appeal enter behavioral scores.
- **Immediate or delayed changes** affect stability, legitimacy, treasury, opinion, unrest, literacy, health, contentment, devastation, infrastructure, research progress, prestige and pluralism. Units are validated, including fractions versus percentage points.
- **Leadership changes** record a named leader or governing body and tenure, replace the leader's persistent temperament, skills, strengths and limitation, and trigger a strategic review. They preserve institutional character and existing state commitments. Individual lifespans and genealogies are not simulated.
- **Cultural legacies** record works, belief movements and institutions. Belief and literature legacies seed small follower or reader communities that may diffuse through contact and trade. Prestige affects migration appeal; pluralism moderates exclusion. Belief populations are explicit aggregate communities; individual readers and physical text inventories are not tracked.

Modifiers can last 1–240 months, with a delay of up to 120 months and steady, fading or growing curves. Expiration removes a modifier without subtracting from an already-mutated base. LLM rate effects begin on the next tick after their event is selected; local and scheduled events enter the monthly lifecycle directly. Governments select responses from current conditions after the initial shock, policy, personality and saved randomness. There are no event-choice cards. Follow-up probability is drawn once, with conditions evaluated over a later window of up to 36 months. Provinces retain place-bound effects through conquest; national effects follow the state.

The chronicle keeps summaries concise. **More detail** generates a cached reading expansion on demand, using the recorded event and effects as facts. This expansion cannot change simulation state or add outcomes. Existing longer event descriptions can be opened without another call.

Saved outcomes, modifier clocks, response choices, follow-up draws and cultural memory continue deterministically given the same external inputs. Future LLM outputs are not guaranteed to repeat, even with a request seed. Native procedural events remain deterministic. Neither browser frame rate nor model latency stops the monthly clock; external inputs are validated against the live world when received and applied.

## State agency, communication and adjudication

Every state identity has persistent private assessment, strategy, goals, memory, institutional compliance, and decision/strategic clocks. Routine decisions default to OpenRouter's `mistralai/ministral-8b-2512`; strategy and referee roles default to the configured DeepSeek event model. The roles can be overridden independently in `.env`. A routine pass handles small domestic adjustments and requests escalation when needed. Strategic reviews become due every 12 simulated months and can also be requested by the lightweight model, new negotiations or the observer. Background calls share a five-second launch spacing and four concurrent worker requests. Each state has a thirty-second minimum launch interval; full strategy passes have an additional two-minute interval, except explicit observer requests. Routine passes reuse unchanged decisions for at least twenty-four simulated months unless hunger, stability, war, leadership, correspondence or proposed agreements change. Due states are scheduled by oldest launch so one state cannot monopolize the service. Eligibility is checked before constructing expensive census/map contexts; outstanding requests are coalesced by state. Deliberation can continue while the monthly world evolves. The current implementation is a hybrid: standing policies and military/logistical rules execute locally while models periodically revise intentions.

An agent receives its own treasury, administrative and operational ledgers, private notes, owned provinces and forces, and its own partial intelligence portfolio. Foreign information is not a live projection of the world. Known states have dated military, economic and political reports with estimated ranges, named sources and confidence that declines with age. Unreported strength, stocks, reserves, intentions and countries are unknown; lack of a report is not evidence of absence. Foreign private character, exact fleet positions, global simulation parameters and organized opposition's exact strengths/countdowns are omitted. Internal resolution reasoning and mechanical effect schedules are not passed back as strategic knowledge. Agents receive implementation status; visible developments and their own administrative changes provide feedback.

Each state begins with local geography, frontier observations and contacts. Armies observe nearby provinces; fleets observe physically nearby coasts. Completed trade routes provide traveled charts and merchant contacts. Allies provide liaison reports. Public news, third-party relationships, broadcasts and agreement snapshots arrive through current contacts after two to six months, rather than appearing globally at creation. Direct correspondence and participant agreements remain immediately available. A public agreement report retains its collection date and can become stale; secret agreements are not intercepted by this implementation. Reporting channels can be lost, while old estimates and charts remain as historical knowledge. Observer UI and the world comparison matrix remain broader than any state's knowledge; the Diplomacy inspector exposes the selected government's actual portfolio.

The standing intelligence allocation defaults to 0.5% of monthly revenue and can range from zero to 15%, limited by actual treasury cash. Collection consumes those funds and increases recorded spending; it creates no people or goods. Networks and collection capacity develop gradually and decline without support. A state can spread effort across contacts or focus on a known rival, with broad, military, economic, political or counterintelligence priorities. Priorities affect collection cadence; networks improve access and report quality, while counterintelligence reduces rival access. Source bias is deterministic per observer, target and field, so repeated context requests cannot reroll a favorable answer. Better sources narrow uncertainty but never grant perfect confidence. Natural local observation continues without an intelligence budget.

Both LLM plans and local standing military strategy use this same portfolio. Enemy-force comparisons, resource leverage and allied deterrence use estimates and reported relationships, including the possibility of missing alliances. Foreign military target selection and automated land path planning use known charts. Local supply, combat and ownership enforcement still resolve against physical reality, allowing surprise and mistaken plans. Domestic authority warnings are qualitative observations rather than exact opposition mobilization or a guaranteed secession countdown. Agents can adjust intelligence through validated domestic orders without waiting for additional provider calls; routine review signals include newly collected reports. Old saves initialize local knowledge without fabricating a history of intelligence investment.

Observer controls can inspect confidential state archives. That capability does not grant other state models access. Agents have no shared conversation or direct world-write capability.

Diplomatic messages can coordinate, threaten, persuade, propose terms or reject them. Offers are immutable and have an engine-issued identity and terms fingerprint. Submission records the author's consent; each other party must accept the exact offer ID and fingerprint. Counteroffers create new documents. Proposal expiry includes a deliberation grace period until invited parties have actually reviewed the offer, so provider latency alone cannot kill a negotiation; ownership, funds and willingness are still checked when replies arrive. The fingerprint is a deterministic binding check, not cryptographic protection against the human owner editing a local save. An agent cannot create another state's signature because the worker binds each response to its actual requesting actor. Ownership, consent and spendable funds are independently enforced by code.

Fully accepted standard transfers, treaties and institutional charters execute through local consent, authority and conservation checks. Territorial, merger and unusual custom offers go to a separate referee model with the authoritative world and consent ledger. It can approve, reject or defer them and describe plausible consequences. Submitted prose and claims are explicitly untrusted. The referee's judgment is an LLM estimate, not a guarantee of realism or immunity to persuasion; deterministic authority and conservation checks remain mandatory after any approval. Deliberate authority violations can reduce legitimacy and institutional compliance. Ordinary diplomatic bluffing, unsuccessful bargaining and genuinely stale ownership information are distinguished from forged authority. New correspondence arriving during deliberation remains unread and can trigger another strategic pass.

### Non-binding observer advice

Each state's agent can receive private observer suggestions through its dossier. Suggestions are data rather than commands: submission does not change autonomy, public correspondence, resources, consent or policies. Agents are instructed to treat the content as opinions and unverified assertions, weigh it against their own motives and intelligence, and provide a concise reply when appropriate. Human-controlled states retain the existing execution guard; advice grants no new authority.

An optional per-agent advisory record stores up to twelve notes, with at most four pending suggestions and 1,200 characters per note. IDs increase within that state's own inbox. Empty and duplicate pending suggestions are rejected. Trimming removes handled history, never pending notes. The selected government sees its pending suggestions and two recent delivered/answered notes (four on strategic passes); other state agents see none. Withdrawal is available until delivery, including while a model request is already in flight.

Pending advice prioritizes its state's next eligible routine assessment, including while the simulation is paused, and changes the routine-cache signal. It retains the shared concurrency, launch-gap and per-state wall-time budgets. It does not force a strategic call; the cheap decision model can respond or request escalation. Each request captures the pending note IDs it actually received. Valid returned plans acknowledge only that captured inbox; newer suggestions remain pending and withdrawn suggestions remain withdrawn. Unknown, duplicate or cross-assessment reply IDs invalidate the plan before any action is applied. A leadership change retains pending advice for the new government's assessment.

Agent plans carry up to four `adviceResponses` with a note ID and at most 400 characters of reply. Older plans without the field remain compatible. A valid assessment with no separate reply marks the note as delivered rather than implying approval. Replies express intentions and judgments, not guaranteed execution. Invalid advice submissions are recoverable UI errors and do not stop playback. Notes, replies and handling dates persist with strict save validation; legacy saves need no advisory migration.

### Diplomatic friction and durable commitments

Each foreign office has finite attention, reduced by pending commitments and replenished over simulated months. Diplomatic skill, administration funding, logistics and domestic stability affect capacity or recovery. Opening a proposal and signing another government's terms spend actual treasury funds and occupy attention. Maintaining active agreements and shared institutions adds monthly overhead and slows recovery; a cash shortage does not silently cancel signed rights. An idle office can devote itself to one unusually large constitutional negotiation, becoming overcommitted rather than making large multilateral settlements categorically impossible. Refusing or withdrawing a pending proposal, and choosing to do nothing, have no diplomatic charge. Scarce attention, repetition or insufficient operating funds produce a practical office note, not an authority violation or simulation pause.

New agreements have an earliest implementation date separate from their response deadline and clause lifetimes. Complexity, participants, known travel distance, logistics and domestic review affect this date, usually several months; even complete consent cannot bypass preparation. Genuine wartime peace/defense negotiations use an expedited one-month path. Unilateral war orders and domestic devolution remain immediately available. The observer's trade, alliance and peace controls submit actual proposals under this same system, with independent native counterpart consent where the government is autonomous. Earlier save documents retain their existing admission contract and gain no retroactive proposal fees or processing delay.

Equivalent proposals are recognized across changed titles, prose, clause order and superficial duration changes. Existing alliance, trade or shared-institution rights do not need another protocol merely to restate them. Ordinary parallel negotiations with the same counterpart consolidate into a counteroffer, and recent or rejected initiatives require time or a material change in leadership, territory, hunger, stability or war. Substantive amendments and genuine reductions in obligations remain possible. Counteroffers require every original participant, and accepted replacements expire the old document's obligations without changing its signed text. Old-source parallel branches cannot multiply an already replaced commitment; cached public treaty privileges are reconciled immediately. Completed asset transfers and institutional acts are not reversed by document replacement; institutions require explicit amendment or withdrawal. Message bandwidth is finite, which discourages acknowledgement loops.

State prompts treat empty correspondence and proposal lists as normal. Foundational rights should generally continue until revoked or replaced, or use justified five-to-ten-year terms; annual strategy review alone is no reason for annual treaty renewal. Existing short contracts are preserved. Local common-threat behavior favors a shared defense league over a simultaneous stack of redundant bilateral defense compacts. The Diplomacy dossier shows attention, foreign-office spending, practical constraints and preparation dates. All constraints run locally without extra model calls.

### Shared institutions and provincial delegation

The World overview exposes the complete relationship matrix and sortable state comparisons for economy, population, military and government, plus provincial comparisons and institutional charters. Confidential institutions require an explicit observer toggle. Institutional templates include defense leagues, customs unions, research communities, confederations, federations and bespoke bodies. Their implemented powers are explicit: common defense and military access, trade preferences, research sharing, contributions and delegated foreign-policy voting. Naming a body a federation does not grant powers outside its charter. Custom ambitions can still be negotiated through ordinary model-mediated offers.

A charter requires exact consent from every founding state. Later admissions and amendments follow its unanimous, majority or population-weighted rule; withdrawal can carry notice-related political costs. Contributions transfer existing national funds into a common treasury, which can issue wartime grants. Research cooperation shares knowledge from funded member institutions. Offensive wars under delegated foreign policy need the charter's votes; defense of an attacked member is authorized by an existing defense commitment. Mergers deduplicate membership, and dissolved institutions refund remaining funds rather than deleting them. Charter sources remain in the reference ledger.

Provincial charters delegate taxation, education, policing and infrastructure with up to 80% revenue autonomy. Retained provincial tax receipts reduce central revenue and enter a persistent local treasury. Local appropriations spend actual retained funds; administration takes one tenth and delegated services divide the remainder. Education, public order/protection and infrastructure respond to their shares of delivered reference-wage labor output. Policing reduces unrest by 0.2 × min(1.5, real policing output per 10,000 residents) monthly. National budgets are consequently constrained by the smaller central revenue base. This is administrative devolution; independently elected provincial agents and legislative institutions are not modeled.

This adjudication layer is internal. State-agent prompts and projected contexts contain no named external referee; practical developments are described as implementation, resistance, material limitations, legal consent and institutional confidence. UI documents use “Terms and developments” and ordinary explanations. New internal reasoning must use this same historical language. Legacy mentions in agreements, correspondence, memories, warnings and chronicle records are translated at presentation/context boundaries. Signed original texts and their digests are never rewritten; autosaves and exports use the raw worker snapshot, independently of the UI projection. Internal adjudication requests and model identifiers are omitted from visible government activity status.

Agents and referee prose cite agreements with `[[agreement:ID]]`, copied from the supplied reference field or a bounded, visibility-filtered citation catalog. The interface resolves tokens into recorded agreement titles. Older explicit references such as “agreement #21” or “proposal 21” are also linked at display time; signed text and digests are never rewritten. Counteroffers expose their `supersedes` link, and correspondence exposes its associated offer. References open an expanded agreement even outside the latest twelve entries, with Back/Forward navigation. Missing or unauthorized references display a generic unavailable label; confidential targets require that state's archive access. Cited ancestors are retained during ledger trimming, including chains through counteroffers, messages, institutional memory and rulings. Citations carry no consent, execution or reactivation semantics, and do not alter numeric protocol IDs or signatures.

Settlement trials isolate the affected bank, credit, cash and inventory ledgers as well as the political records. Local market depots remain the same physical stores exposed by provincial views. A successful territorial settlement commits those linked views together; a later failing clause leaves the prior balances and claims intact.

Supported settlement mechanisms include one-time payments, recurring tribute, physical goods supply obligations, preferential trade, alliances, peace, nonaggression, military access, guarantees, research cooperation, province transfers, territorial purchases, state formation, mergers and declarations of war. Unconventional terms can be expressed freely as custom clauses; the referee maps them into supported operations and bounded effects or explains why they cannot be carried out. It cannot execute arbitrary code or simply pretend an unsupported physical action happened. Custom financial consequences must resolve to explicit conserved transfers, not treasury multipliers.

Payments, territory, state creation and mergers execute as one transaction after rechecking the live world. Transfers and mergers conserve civilian and military population and treasury funds. Mergers carry treasury, debt and armies into the successor; state formation requires real founding provinces and funding. Cultural identity is retained rather than instantly assimilated. Capitals are relocated when needed. Extinct identities remain in historical arrays, with up to 256 historical state identities supported. Voluntary sovereignty transfers require all relevant parties' consent; ordinary conquest still uses the military model.

A recurring goods clause is a real logistical obligation, prioritized before ordinary market clearing and sharing the same tile and connection capacity. It draws on actual surplus, reserves, reachable buyers and real carriage; promises do not teleport or create goods. Payments for goods are separate agreed terms. Tribute transfers available treasury funds monthly and records shortfalls. Research cooperation shares bounded knowledge subject to the source's funding. Secret trade/access/alliance rights affect the relevant mechanics without automatically becoming public treaty labels. Term expiration and obligation shortfalls are retained for subsequent negotiation. Guarantees and political promises inform agent strategy; they do not automatically spawn armies or declare a new war.

Model responses are checked against current ownership and assets, and outstanding requests are canceled when a different world is generated or loaded. The worker owns playback timing and yields after short slices (one expensive month may exceed the slice budget). World snapshots are coalesced to two per second above 12×, and five per second otherwise; pause flushes the latest world. Pending external requests do not gate ticks. The requested pace is a CPU-bounded target, not a guarantee of sixty months per second for arbitrarily large worlds. Background snapshots do not acknowledge unrelated commands. Autosaves check for changed state every fifteen seconds and coalesce pending captures. The repository validation record distinguishes completed core regressions and endurance scenarios from unqualified live-provider and adversarial-agent behavior.

## Government and insolvency

Income taxes are withheld from actual private and public wage payments. Positive producer cash operating surplus is taxed after credit service. The collected rate is the policy tax rate times collection capacity: clamp((0.6 + 0.045 × governance technology) × (0.5 + stability/200) × tax-effect multiplier, 0, 1). This preserves governance, leadership and historical effects without collecting more than the statutory rate or inventing receipts. Domestic and international goods purchases transfer buyer funds to a seller; import tariffs transfer funds to the destination treasury. No output-derived tax revenue is minted.

Welfare, education, military and research sliders are relative weights. Administration and infrastructure retain weights 12 and 18. The desired monthly envelope is max(0.95 × tax receipts, 0.00002 × civilian population) + 0.02 × max(0, treasury − 12 × tax receipts), funded by treasury and available credit. The reserve rule releases 2% of cash above a twelve-month revenue buffer; it prevents perpetual hoarding of public surpluses without spending unavailable funds. Welfare transfers cash to households, with a need weighting and an owner weight of 0.1. Public-service allocations buy available labor; a common staffing fill ratio distributes a labor shortage across service categories, so the order in which categories are processed cannot starve administration or research. An appropriation alone supplies no service. Reported service budgets are actual delivered amounts, except military: half its appropriation is retained as a funded operations envelope for recruitment and supplies. Only the staffed share produces civilian military services. Provincial services use the same labor and settlement constraints.

Welfare can improve purchases and contentment only when paid; it is a transfer, not a second purchase of medical workers. Education, basic public-health administration, infrastructure and research use delivered labor valued at the reference wage, not nominal appropriations. Provincial service output devotes 10% to administration and splits 90% among delegated non-tax powers. A change in the unit of currency cannot buy more teachers, maintenance or knowledge. Welfare's direct contentment proxy is deflated by the food price; household wealth is deflated by the basket price. Military service funding and separately purchased supplies retain their existing military rules. Infrastructure maintenance uses I[t+1] = clamp(I[t] + 0.005 × real infrastructure-service output per 10,000 people − 0.0015 × I[t] − 0.0007 × devastation, 0.08, 1.5), alongside the separate material-shortage damage and funded construction commands. Wear is proportional to the infrastructure stock; an absolute monthly deduction previously overwhelmed ordinary paid maintenance. These are index units, not a measured kilometer-by-kilometer engineering depreciation schedule. Military goods, recruitment, diplomacy and other government expenses settle to counterpart accounts.

Commercial-bank lending, amortization, restructuring and bank resolution follow the balance-sheet rules above. Welfare remains limited by actual treasury deposits and approved borrowing; a policy appropriation alone creates no household income or service.

## Research

Each of six fields has six levels. Required knowledge is `140 × (level + 1)^1.45`. Real delivered research labor produces knowledge, modified by literacy and governance technology: monthly progress = reference-wage research output / 7 × (0.35 + literacy) × (1 + 0.07 × governance level) × research effect. Focus changes preserve all field progress.

A research community shares 0.004 times its members' combined current real research output equally, up to 12 knowledge units per recipient per month. Bilateral research agreements transfer the smaller of their agreed knowledge amount and the donor's previous-month real research output / 20. The previous month is explicit because agreements settle before current production; resetting monthly service counters cannot erase that output. Diplomatic staffing also uses real administrative output: staff = min(1.4, real administration per 100,000 residents × 0.04); office capacity = 3 + 0.8 × diplomatic skill + 0.2 × logistics level + staff. None of these quantities increases solely because wages and appropriations are expressed in larger currency units.

| Field       | Implemented effects per completed level                            |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Agriculture | +19% base farm productivity                                        |
| Industry    | +13% base overall production efficiency                            |
| Medicine    | +0.05 health-target contribution                                   |
| Logistics   | +25% base road throughput                                          |
| Military    | +16% combat-effectiveness contribution, plus morale support        |
| Governance  | Better tax collection, literacy growth, and research effectiveness |

Discoveries are named in `TECH_NAMES`. Their labels describe an abstract progression; e.g. “Steam shipping” is not a separate naval vessel system.

## Diplomacy and warfare

Each unordered pair of states has opinion, treaty, grievance, war exhaustion, and a transition date. Treaty states are neutral, trade, alliance, war, or truce. International commerce gradually improves opinion.

- A diplomatic mission costs 150k crowns and adds 12 opinion points while at peace.
- Trade agreements require opinion ≥10; alliances require ≥45.
- Alliances and common-defense institutions grant access. Autonomous allies assess actual combined strength and risk tolerance before military intervention; a human-controlled cabinet is never signed up automatically.
- Native offensive proposals initialize their own diplomacy records; they do not require optional model agents to have been enabled. Quarterly reviews check dates crossed by the integration interval, so Max speed does not skip cabinets. Local standing cabinets mobilize on military threat and make quarterly offensive assessments using ambition, dominance, opportunism, force superiority, allied deterrence, food, stability, funds and seeded chance. These coexist with model strategy and execute without waiting for provider responses. Shared-threat neighbors can form bilateral pacts or multilateral defense leagues; small adjacent peers with a domestic union mandate can propose a survival merger, which still requires consent and territorial adjudication.
- Exhaustion accumulates in war and rises faster with food shortages. Native autonomous belligerents settle at a character-dependent threshold of 30–50; connected state agents negotiate their settlements.
- Manual early peace proposals need exhaustion ≥5 when relations are deeply hostile. An accepted truce prevents renewed war for 60 months.

### Land forces, supply and civilian relations

Winning a battle establishes occupation rather than instant social integration. Local allegiances persist; occupation incurs administration costs, resistance and an unrest floor. Physical garrisons, provision and political allegiance matter for consolidation. Existing small defenders do not suppress local levies: willing residents can reinforce defense using real adults and arms, with allegiance to the invader reducing willingness. Organized, insufficiently suppressed resistance can restore its former sovereign identity instead of always creating another state. Integration requires at least 36 peaceful qualifying months with low resistance and substantial local allegiance.

Armies carry food and arms rather than drawing unlimited provisions from their current province. They aim to carry three months of food and four months of arms, consuming 34 kg food and 0.5 kg arms per soldier per month, with further arms expenditure in battles. Supply depots are existing provincial inventories: the capital, or sufficiently developed, undamaged infrastructure/industry hubs. Sources must be owned; transit requires sovereign, allied or negotiated military access. Hostile territory breaks a line. Distance, unrest and devastation reduce throughput. Supply uses actual physical road/sea paths and the capacity left after civilian freight; shipments are recorded and visible on the military map. Loading transfers real goods into carried inventories. These stores must be included in goods accounting. Supplies remain an aggregate monthly logistics flow rather than individually simulated wagons or ships' manifests.

Reserves buffer interrupted deliveries. Sustained shortage reduces morale, increases attrition, and eventually encourages desertion. Soldiers retain their population identities, mother tongue and civic allegiances. Shared language helps coordination; loyalty, funded defense support, fatigue and provision affect cohesion and behavior, without innate cultural combat coefficients. The existing payroll support proxy is clamp(delivered military-service budget / max(1, military personnel × 0.008), 0, 1); this expenditure pays civilian defense-service workers. Separate soldier bank accounts and individual military wages are not represented. Deserters return to local civilian communities instead of disappearing or being counted as deaths. Severe disaffection can cause temporary refusal of orders. Dead troops enter the death ledger, and surrendered forces return to the civilian census; detailed prisoner-of-war camps are not simulated.

Army standing orders select marching, recovery or entrenchment. Terrain-weighted road effort, logistics technology, cohesion, fatigue and supply determine progress; movement is no longer a guaranteed province per month. March orders follow land only. Defensive preparation takes time and is lost on movement. Mountains multiply defense by 1.8; hills/forests by 1.3, with additional river-crossing and amphibious penalties. Battles use real field forces and local levies: raising a levy removes adults and arms from the province. Casualties, retreats, occupation damage and collateral civilian deaths change the actual population ledger. Conquest preserves identities and allegiances; civilian treatment changes grievance and cooperation. A requisition order may divert up to a quarter of monthly local household food needs when ordinary provision fails, increasing local grievance, unrest and damage. No free civilian provisions are created.

### Standing establishments and replacement personnel

Army creation, save validation and naval passenger manifests share a structural limit of 16,384 army records. Recruitment checks that boundary before spending funds, arms or civilians; mobilization, local levies, secession guards and replacement cadres use the same bound. This replaces the obsolete 500-formation limit that rejected otherwise valid larger worlds. Imports retain every valid formation below the shared bound; they do not discard or merge troops to fit an older limit.

Army formations retain an authorized establishment. Explicit new corps and local levies record their original muster; older depleted formations acquire a nominal establishment of at least 3,000 without gaining people. Monthly replacements are capped by that establishment, a policy/population-based national target, 4% of establishment, local adult/training capacity, recruitment willingness, available arms/food, treasury and 15% of the military appropriation. Each actual replacement costs 0.2 thousand crowns and 0.012 t of arms, and carries 0.04 t of provisions taken from local stocks. Profiles transfer from civilian cohorts and dilute formation experience; neither retirement nor replacement changes identities or creates people. Replenishment requires adequate supply, an owned local province, food coverage, no mutinous disorder and no embarkation. It operates under standing military policy even while domestic policy is manually controlled, and does not wait for a model call.

A state with military funding/conscription but no surviving land formation can rebuild a paid 100-person cadre at a provisioned owned province, then expand through the same constrained replacement process. It receives no free troops or equipment. Underfunded, isolated or starving forces can still shrink; the protection/authority system gives that weakness political consequences. The army inspector exposes establishment and replacements this month.

### Fleets and sea control

Navies contain warships and transports, crews, hull condition, morale, provisions, arms and a water-cell position. Commissioning at an owned harbor consumes treasury, timber, iron, tools, armament and local adult crews. New worlds attempt to fund one starter fleet per coastal state from existing resources; legacy saves acquire no free ships or people. Construction is currently an immediate investment abstraction. A state may operate eight fleets, with commissions limited to 16 ships. A warship needs 90 crew and a transport 30. Crew recruitment, aging, retirement, losses, demobilization, state mergers and population identities share the military census. Purchased supplies, hull wear and paid repairs limit operation. Allied/negotiated port access allows resupply and repair services; foreign provisions and repair services are paid, and replacement crews are recruited only at owned ports.

Courses use connected adjacent water cells, with movement constrained by supply, crew availability, hull and logistics technology. Missions include patrol, escort, blockade, troop transport and return. Nearby hostile fleets fight using ship mix, hull, ammunition, crew readiness, supply, morale, command effects and technology. Damage, lost ships, crew deaths and lost passenger capacity have physical consequences. There are no tactical gun arcs, individual hull classes, weather fronts or rescue simulation; fleet combat is aggregate.

Warships project sea control only across nearby connected water cells. Hostile superiority reduces shipping throughput and can close a corridor; friendly/allied warships counter it. Civilian trade and military sea supply use this control. Military sea supply additionally needs an actual owned escort/transport fleet on the relevant sea route and shares its available cargo budget across deliveries. Embarked troops reduce cargo capacity. Monthly convoy capacity is abstracted; merchant hull ownership and individual supply voyages are not simulated.

An army can embark only at the same accessible harbor as an owned fleet, with sufficient berths. An intact transport carries 800 troops; warships have small incidental transport capacity. The embarked army remains separate from crew in the census, disappears from land combat, consumes its own provisions and moves with the fleet. Friendly or returning landings disembark it; hostile landings must win an amphibious battle. Lost transports can kill embarked troops. Shipwreck survival and naval prisoners are not modeled. State agents receive force readiness, port locations, fleet capabilities and naval orders; military access and sovereign authority remain checked by code.

Unrest reflects contentment, social grievance, loyalty, exclusion, occupation and devastation. The former random six-month breakaway roll has been replaced by a persistent provincial authority assessment. Civil protection depends on stability, legitimacy, infrastructure, local consent, actually delivered administrative/policing labor, unrest and food. A peaceful consenting province does not require a standing garrison to avoid automatically becoming a rebellion. Supplied, cohesive loyal forces contribute locally and at reduced strength in neighboring provinces; infrastructure, terrain and devastation limit that reach. Actual hostile forces and organized domestic challengers undermine protection.

A province tracks inadequate protection, dominant political movement, organization, support, estimated mobilizable people and months of contested authority. For each community, dissent propensity is clamp(((grievance + 1−loyalty + unrest/100)/3 − 0.35)/0.65,0,1). The dominant opposition movement is selected by dissent-weighted adult membership, not total identity membership. Its share is dissent-weighted adults divided by all adults. Support adjusts 8% monthly toward the province's corresponding aggregate propensity. Organization adjusts 6% toward clamp(sqrt(dissent share) × (0.5 + 0.25 × literacy + 0.15 × rootedness) − min(0.2, loyal strength/(0.02 × population)),0,1). Shared identity among contented citizens therefore does not manufacture an armed opposition. Mobilizable numbers remain limited by real adults and equipment. Sustained protection below 50% raises unrest and erodes loyalty after three months. Governments and the chronicle receive escalating warnings; state contexts expose emergencies and conditional time to possible loss of control, and severe new contests can request strategic review within the existing call budget.

The civil-protection component is clamp(0.2 + 0.002 × stability + 0.0025 × legitimacy + 0.15 × infrastructure + 0.35 × loyalty + 0.15 × (1−exp(−real administration-and-policing output/max(1,0.00001 × population)))), multiplied by (1−0.3 × unrest/100) × (0.75+0.25 × food coverage). Existing hostile and organized armed challenges erode this component relative to local security need; supplied loyal forces can restore protection. This separates civic consent and service capacity from military occupation. The [World Bank's social-contract framework](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/futureofgovernment/social-contract-role) motivates that distinction; the coefficients remain synthetic and are not a fitted theory of secession.

A challenge needs organization ≥55%, provincial opposition support ≥45%, at least 75 mobilizable people, unrest ≥60, six months of inadequate protection and mobilization potential above 1.2× effective loyal strength. Three consecutive contested months and twelve months under the current owner allow a transition. Recovery reduces neglect/contestation rather than forcing a scripted collapse. Mobilization draws actual adults from the supporting political movement and consumes existing arms or, for at most 300 people, tools/timber for improvised equipment. No military people or starting treasury are created.

An organized coalition can found a regional state, join an adjacent successor with the same movement and predecessor, or replace a government reduced to one province. A capable foreign force already physically present can obtain control through a sufficiently strong local challenge; a remote declaration cannot annex territory. New states inherit institutions/technology, receive only a bounded transfer of existing provincial/public funds, and must support their own guard. Lost capitals relocate. A government without territory capitulates and its remaining military profiles return to the local civilian census. Political and historical identity labels are retained rather than forcibly assimilated. The identity capacity is 256 across persistence and model schemas; at that hard limit a new peripheral sovereign identity cannot be allocated, while supported absorption and capital-level government replacement remain representable.

## Long-run validation and remaining limitations

Under model version 4, three Regional resident-district scenarios completed 2,400 monthly steps and exact save restoration with identical continuation through month 2,401. Population changed by +32.6%, +31.1% and −5.2%; the eight opening states finished as eight, eight and ten. Monthly census, money, banking, goods and GDP reconciliation checks passed.

These are broad diagnostic passes, not calibrated forecasts or proof of a stationary equilibrium. Across the last 30 annual observations, population contracts by 0.13–0.41% per year and prices deflate by 0.63–4.52% per year. Earlier price indices peak at 6.62–8.33 times their reference level. The version-4 wage minimum bound 8.4–38.7% of private employment at the endpoints. Version 5 replaces that wage rule, so these older runs do not qualify the current labor and monetary mechanics. Full trajectories, food coverage, acceptance thresholds and source fingerprints are in the validation record. The 200-year runs use frozen baseline geography; concurrent terrain changes have separate, shorter compatibility checks.

## Persistence and performance boundaries

The worker owns mutable simulation state. The UI receives snapshots and sends typed commands. Save validation checks dimensions, collections, identifiers, population, stocks, policies, diplomacy, armies, routes, events, and history before accepting an import. Imports support worlds up to 384 MiB. Exact dated cohorts and economic accounts increase checkpoint size; earlier provincial-model checkpoints reached about 126 MB after one year. Resident districts add records and require separate capacity qualification.

Recent history retains monthly points for ten years and annual points before that. The chronicle retains the latest 240 events; event identifiers continue increasing. Autosave checks for changes every fifteen seconds and flushes when the page becomes hidden. The simulation worker validates and captures immutable save bytes synchronously so one save cannot mix two simulation months. This capture temporarily occupies the worker; disk and browser writes then run asynchronously through a separate storage channel. One write runs at a time, with no queue of full world copies. A replaced world cannot receive stale save-success notifications. Tab closure may interrupt its final asynchronous flush; the last completed checkpoint remains authoritative.

Local preview/development saves to `.uchrony/autosaves/`, excluded from Git, with separate browser save slots identified by a local cookie. Disk writes use private temporary files, a checksum, atomic rename, file/directory synchronization and one previous verified copy. An unreadable current file falls back to that previous copy; damaged files are not copied over a good backup. Same-origin checks protect save writes, slot IDs cannot select arbitrary paths, and snapshots contain no API credentials. IndexedDB stores a second copy and provides a fallback when the disk service is unavailable. Browser fallback saves are retried against disk even while paused. Startup reads the available replica envelopes, then validates and expands candidates from newest to oldest until one succeeds. It does not retain two decoded worlds merely to choose a timestamp; a corrupt newer copy still falls back to an older valid one. Neither backend uses the old size-limited `localStorage` world slot.

The old browser save remains intact and is read as a legacy fallback. `/save-recovery` can copy and verify that legacy save on disk without starting a simulation. Failed restoration no longer generates and autosaves a replacement world automatically. If both write backends fail, the UI reports the actual failure, retries, and keeps Export available. A successful retry clears the warning. Export remains the portable cross-browser workflow; local save slots are not cloud synchronization.

The deterministic core is reproducible for the same build, starting save, commands and recorded external historical inputs; future LLM outputs are not guaranteed to repeat. Rendering frame rate never changes random state. Background tab throttling changes wall-clock playback pace; it does not simulate elapsed real time while closed.

Meaningful future additions include subsurface exploration and ore grades, individual households, bank choice and deposit interest, exchange rates, asset markets, individual convoy voyages, ship classes and naval weather, detailed prisoners/rescue, formal war goals, and empirical calibration.

## Computation and scheduling

Migration evaluates simultaneous plans using per-district culture, language and belief marginals instead of rescanning every destination community for each sort comparison. Corridors use compact undirected hex-edge indices; phase profiles share physical link costs and capacities across military views. Supply and movement graphs are shared per state within the army phase and invalidated on ownership changes. Maritime replanning stops once the required destination ports have settled shortest paths, and plans still inside their minimum adoption period avoid redundant replanning. Provincial shortest paths use a heap. Tariff preferences are prepared once per market-clearing phase; civilian and military traffic still share actual physical capacities. These are computational changes, not coarse time steps: demographic, production, consumption, research, obligations and military effects continue monthly.

### World-scale geography and markets

Province road trees occupy disjoint cells and share one map-sized distance/predecessor allocation. Above 300 provinces, initial shipping links come from a multi-source sailing-distance Voronoi graph: neighboring harbor catchments connect through water, while land links and a spanning selection preserve access across navigable basins. Subsequent corridor replans retain those port locations and connection pairs, along with existing path inertia. Province grouping is a single pass over land cells. State and province names extend beyond the original twelve states and 384 place-name combinations.

Large-world initial populations scale with province land area. Initial standing forces recruit 0.2% of each state's civilians where available (minimum target 3,000), drawing actual adults from its provinces. Regional generation preserves its original initialization. No personnel are added outside the population ledger.

Monthly local trade caches demand targets, physical access and supplier paths across goods. Purchase credit is requested only after available seller stock and remaining shared transport capacity limit the executable order; a blocked shipment cannot create a new loan. Only funded shipments materialize their complete journey legs; candidate paths combine precomputed edge-use counts, including repeated traversals. Exhausted paths are rejected for the remainder of that month. Bilateral tariff rates are prepared once per clearing phase. A failed loan limit is remembered only within that phase, during which outstanding principal can only rise and bank lending capacity can only fall; a higher requested limit still invokes the bank checks. This avoids millions of identical unsuccessful credit requests without lending additional money or skipping a funded purchase. In the legacy provincial market routine (before district simulation), above 300 provinces merchants consider a finite number of new supplier contacts per good each month: 16 plus infrastructure × 16 and logistics level × 8, rounded. Existing supplier relationships remain eligible beyond this search allowance. Suppliers are ranked by transport effort with the existing domestic preference, so nearby and efficient sea connections shape regional markets; a scarce good may still have a distant specialist as its closest available supplier. Contracts continue to use their explicit delivery rules. Naval control is evaluated against a phase-local diplomatic view, and land-only links skip naval calculations.

The atlas caps its terrain raster's longest side at 2,400 pixels and paints exact boundaries into a viewport-sized cache. Relief, rivers, labels, picking and all thematic layers retain world coordinates. Rivers below the current visual scale disappear at overview distance. Geography fingerprints are computed when the worker generates or restores a world; subsequent display messages omit the immutable cells, and the UI reunites them for rendering. Saves and exports come from the complete authoritative worker state, never the display projection.

### Display snapshots

The view always retains exact regional identity/age censuses, physical map summaries, national accounts and global bank balances and loan totals. Household balances, local stratum counts, firm inventories, employment and credit contracts are subscribed for the selected state only; other states load when selected. A pending dossier explicitly shows loading instead of fabricated zero balances. This subscription is UI-only, is never saved, and cannot consume simulation randomness or change economic resolution. It sends exact per-market arriving/leaving freight totals instead of every local shipment path. Transaction work ledgers, inventory valuation workbooks and production forecasts remain in the simulation and saves; unused work buffers are empty in the view and share one immutable-by-convention inventory object across the message. Full identity censuses are not duplicated in every local-market record. The `viewOnly` marker prevents saving or restoring such a projection as an authoritative world. The application advances only the canonical worker state. Regional inspection remains a separate bounded request. Exact trajectory comparisons must pin both the source and JavaScript runtime. The tested Node and Chromium versions differ at floating-point precision in `log`, `exp`, `pow` and `hypot`; the same initial saved values therefore do not imply bit-identical future trajectories across engines. Observer-independence tests use the same runtime and retain exact comparisons, without rounding away discrepancies. Residential display records carry site identity, physical position/footprint, occupied population, market and capacity in an ordered array; the full site registry remains canonical. The renderer shards large string lookups, keeps insertion order, and writes overview buildings directly into instance buffers without a second object graph. These allocation changes preserve geometry and do not alter economic state.

### Lossless census storage

Resident economies retain exact dated cohorts in memory. Save files store repeated community field names once in a versioned schema table and preserve each community’s original field order and values. They encode each cohort list as a versioned base64 sequence of unsigned variable-length integers: the row count, zigzag-encoded changes in birth month, and exact female/male counts. Decoding restores the original tuple order and every integer before ordinary census/account validation. There is no age averaging, cohort truncation or rounding. Earlier tuple-array saves remain readable. Checksums cover the saved representation, and imported malformed, truncated, excessive-length or overflowing encoded cohorts are rejected. Each province census uses an independent standard gzip/base64 block. Version 2 partitions the large economic and local-market registries into blocks of at most 256 records, preserving collection type and key order; nested ledger, inventory, loan and ownership tables are also bounded. Version-1 whole-registry blocks remain readable. Every block declares its decoded length and CRC. Decompression uses bounded output buffers; malformed lengths, checksums and unsupported formats fail before model validation. Serialization also walks nested compressed containers rather than flattening a whole registry into a temporary JSON string. Each bounded byte chunk uses one Blob backing segment, avoiding hundreds of thousands of tiny segments. This avoids a single enormous world JSON string. Earlier uncompressed and census-only saves remain readable. The 384 MiB file limit is unchanged; lossless compression makes room for district accounts and demographic detail.

### Adaptive save migration

Adaptive topology, sites, jurisdiction succession, construction, commuting and freight-in-transit are versioned authoritative records. Site, jurisdiction, history, route and compact commuting collections use independent gzip blocks of at most 4,096 entries; original key order is restored for exact continuation. Decoded-size, checksum, duplicate identity, topology, land coverage, ownership and date checks run before accepting a save. Visual layouts and representative-day snapshots are disposable.

The first successful disk save replacing a pre-adaptive world preserves the exact previous file as `<slot>.pre-adaptive-v1.json`, separately from the rotating previous checkpoint. Migration initializes absent capacity with recorded assumptions; it does not manufacture people, cash, mineral reserves, goods or loan forgiveness. Existing exports remain loadable. The 384 MiB saved-file boundary remains in force; exceeding it is an explicit save error, never population or account truncation.

## Frontier competition and extinction

The selected `expansionist` profile ranks economic frontier value at three times the baseline weight and adds peer pressure from received intelligence. Prosperity gaps, reported territorial gaps and expansion reports are weighted 0.45, 0.35 and 0.20; distant contacts carry half the weight of neighbors. Economic confidence decays as `exp(-age/36)` and territorial-chart confidence as `exp(-age/120)`. Hidden accounts never enter the comparison. Own shortages and population pressure also contribute. These signals affect real budget priorities, retained expedition savings, settlement reviews and military motives; they do not create money, population, goods or automatic conquest.

Automatic pioneers number 120, leave at least 300 residents and half a month of food at home, and carry proportional supplies paid from actual stocks. Quarterly reviews consider up to eight sites per neighboring independent province within a 40-cell accessible overland range. Accession needs four years of contact and eighteen months of sustained support. City-state prosperity needs three years, plus the population, food, order, relative wealth and funded charter requirements documented in the README. Entrepreneurship is allowed above 60 residents, with equity `max(0.025, min(1, (population/500)^2), population*0.00015)` and at least twice that amount held by the investing owner. Larger towns retain the ordinary capital floor.

True extinction requires zero civilians plus zero military residents. It ends government activity, claims and active diplomacy; institutions remove extinct members and dissolve if fewer than two remain. Historical state IDs, buildings and financial claims are retained to preserve history and accounting. Empty cities are recorded as abandoned; falling below the 500-person naming threshold does not erase a smaller surviving population. Taking observer control cannot reactivate an extinct government.

The reproducible profile comparison, demographic ablation, conservation checks and remaining limits are recorded in [the 27 October tuning report](validation/expansion-tuning-2026-10-27.md).


## Late-game decision policy (model 6)

The default `dynamic` policy uses merchant surplus release 0.02564/month, work disutility 0.40758, investment cash share 0.23396, and investment adjustment 0.03502/month. These four weights were selected by a deterministic seven-candidate offline evolutionary search. Food satiation is 1.5 subsistence baskets; standing readiness is 1% of population before conscription/policy modifiers; the offensive score threshold is 0.8. Military and physical constraints were not optimized for war counts. The comparison profiles remain available only to explicit experiments.

Automatic pioneers and military recruitment each carry six months of current domestic wages per person as their labor obligation, in addition to physical supply constraints. Cadres can begin with 20 actual adults. Armies procure and reinforce before retail consumption. Payroll readiness follows current wages and the integration interval. Frontier reviews exclude occupied sites before limiting their candidate count. These changes apply to resumed saves without fabricating resources or resurrecting extinct states.

The reward, failed trials, source fingerprints, long runs and limitations are in [the late-game validation report](validation/late-dynamics-2026-10-27.md). This is synthetic policy calibration, not a fine-tuned language model or empirical historical calibration.


## Development programs, productive reserves and public credit (model 8)

Native governments retain five-year development objectives, with a target of 10% more real output per resident. They prioritize food security, industrial supplies, education or transport according to domestic conditions. Research is produced by paid researchers; the revised conversion is 0.01 progress points per base-wage-equivalent researcher-month before literacy and institutional modifiers. Targets do not award goods, cash or technology. Human governments and recently accepted external-agent plans retain control.

Budgets use a smoothed current tax/dividend base and a six-month cash buffer. Bank borrowing remains constrained by bank capital, three years of current revenue and a debt-service allowance of 20% of revenue. Public bank principal amortizes by 1/240 of the outstanding balance per month; this is a declining-balance repayment rate, not a guaranteed 20-year maturity. Debt pressure tightens spending; unemployment raises the recovery appropriation. These constraints can still prevent poor or indebted governments from funding their ambitions.

States can fund farm equipment, tool workshops and textile workshops. The former fixed 900k industry price is replaced by local construction wages and materials, with fractional projects and six-month completion. Governments subscribe new enterprise equity at book value rather than expropriating existing shares. Equipment improves productive capacity; the enterprise still needs workers, input supplies, customers and working capital. Taxes and dividends reach the treasury only through actual payments.

A strategic reserve policy raises targeted inventories of timber, stone, iron, coal and tools to at least six months of expected sales (the existing coarse-period operating buffer can be larger). Efficient new production retained in an unfilled reserve can earn up to 25% relief on the enterprise's profit tax, weighted by its useful strategic output. Sold output and old stock alone earn no reserve relief. Below 97% food coverage, native productive investment prioritizes farm equipment and strategic reserve incentives pause until food security recovers; short-term labor priorities can change without abandoning the five-year objective. Finite targets, financing, extraction limits and storage losses remain binding. Existing trade opens larger markets; unsold stocks never become treasury revenue merely because a route could eventually sell them.

Farm and productive-enterprise projects can use surplus materials from local private merchants on supplier credit when their cash allocation is insufficient. Ordinary consumption, input reserves and two periods of observed demand are protected. Wages and taxes remain payable in crowns. Each deferred purchase records a compensation claim owned by the supplier and an equal public liability; no crowns are created. Supplier credit is limited to twelve months of the current revenue base and amortizes by 1/60 of the outstanding balance per month, subject to available treasury funds. Unpaid claims persist, follow market splits/mergers and legal state succession, and reduce capacity for further borrowing. Repayment transfers existing cash and does not count as a second capital expense. This mechanism currently covers farmland and productive-enterprise construction; other procurement paths retain their existing rules.

The overview separates nominal monthly value added, constant-price output per resident, public revenue minus accrued spending, enterprise investment, enterprise dividends, cash reserves and outstanding supplier debt. The balance before enterprise investment adds back only that identified investment category; it is not a complete government operating-account measure. Principal repayments reduce cash and debt, not current expense. A negative value-added figure is not a treasury deficit, and an unsold material reserve is not a cash surplus.

## Military control and negotiated settlements

Battle victory establishes temporary military control while legal ownership remains with the defender. Victory and continued control share a population-scaled garrison requirement: at least 20 effective troops or 0.1% of the province's population, whichever is larger. Supply and morale reduce effective strength; winning combat is still required. This replaces a fixed 200-soldier victory cutoff that excluded small frontier armies. Control ends when the force withdraws or loses control. Occupied provinces stop remitting ordinary taxes to their legal owner. Annexation is a separate government action after six months of secure control and funded administration; it creates resistance, grievances and military influence. Native governments make this decision according to their ambitions and domestic capacity.

An occupying army may pillage. The levy transfers existing local merchant cash and a proportional portion of the defender's treasury to the occupying government, once per province per accounting period. Supply and occupation strength limit it. Devastation, grievances, fear and opposition rise, reducing the territory's future value. Splitting an army cannot collect the same levy repeatedly.

Peace proposals can combine a five-year truce, one-time compensation and territorial concessions. Each government signs for itself; human governments are never automatically signed. Native cabinets compare their own exhaustion, occupied land and financial burden. Preparation time still applies. Cash and ownership are rechecked at settlement, failed settlements roll back atomically, and armies withdraw from territory not ceded. Military control alone never becomes a peace border.
